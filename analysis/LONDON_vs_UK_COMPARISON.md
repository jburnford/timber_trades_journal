### Key Findings:

**Liverpool was MORE dependent on New York:**
- Peak dependence: 12.7% (vs London's 8.2%)

- Liverpool maintained higher NY share even in decline

**The collapse was NATIONWIDE:**
- Every major port saw New York decline
- Liverpool's absolute decline (-5.2pts) less than London (-6.0pts)

**Grimsby was immune:**
- Never depended on New York (1.4% max)
- Focused on Baltic bulk timber
- Mining supplies from Scandinavia

### What Did New York Ship to Each Port?

**New York's Commodity Mix:**
- **Liverpool**: Staves, oak, lumber, mahogany (diverse high-value)
- **London**: Staves, doors, walnut, mouldings (finished + hardwoods)
- **Bristol**: Deals, boards (more traditional)
- **Grimsby**: Minimal (never a major supplier)

**Why Liverpool Stayed Higher:**
- Maintained stave trade longer (cooperage industry)
- Hardwood imports for furniture makers
- More diverse NY commodity mix

## Historical Interpretation

### The Late Victorian Timber Trade Restructured Regionally

**Before 1870s**:
- All ports similar (bulk timber imports)
- London dominant by volume
- Suppliers = Canada, Baltic, Norway interchangeably

**By 1890s**:
- **Functional specialization** by port
- **Geographic specialization** by supplier
- **Commodity specialization** by trade route

### Why Did This Happen?

**1. Transportation Revolution:**
- **Railways** created inland distribution networks
- **Steamships** made regular schedules profitable
- **Direct routes** developed (e.g., Gothenburg → Grimsby for mining)

**2. Industrial Geography:**
- **Coal mining** (Midlands) → Grimsby bulk imports
- **Shipbuilding** (Tyne, Clyde) → Norwegian spar specialists
- **Furniture** (London) → Swedish finished goods
- **Construction** (London) → Value-added imports

**3. Imperial/Colonial Decline:**
- **Canadian squared timber trade** ended (Navy stopped using wood ships)
- **American hardwoods** redirected to US domestic market
- **British focus** shifted to European suppliers (closer, cheaper)

**4. Second Industrial Revolution:**
- **Processing moved upstream** (cut at source, not destination)
- **Standardization** enabled (doors/mouldings in standard sizes)
- **Vertical integration** (Swedish mills controlled supply chain)

### London's Unique Position

**London alone** transformed into a **value-added market** because:

1. **Largest consumer market** justified premium imports
2. **Highest construction values** paid for finished goods
3. **Best infrastructure** handled diverse cargo types
4. **Established relationships** with Swedish manufacturers
5. **Capital availability** for wholesale/distribution

**Other ports served INDUSTRIAL needs** (raw materials):
- Coal mining (props)
- Shipbuilding (timber, spars)
- Manufacturing (general lumber)
- These needed **volume**, not **value-added**
