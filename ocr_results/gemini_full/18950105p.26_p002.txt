GILLETT, WILLIAM HENRY, builder, South Avenue, Sutton Road, Southend, lately residing at Grove Terrace, Church End, Finchley, and afterwards at Ardleigh Villas, Kilworth Avenue, Southend—The statutory meeting of the creditors interested under this failure took place on Tuesday at the offices of the Official Receiver for the Chelmsford district, Temple Chambers, E.C., under the presidency of Mr. Shirtcliffe, Assistant Official Receiver. The statement of affairs disclosed gross liabilities amounting to £1,215 19s. 9d., of which £254 19s. 9d. was due to unsecured creditors: to fully secured creditors £961, the value of the security being valued at £1 200, thus showing an estimated surplus of £239 to rank as an asset. The assets consist of the estimated surplus of £239, and good book debts £12 16s. 5d.; total assets, £251 16s. 5d.; from which £14 17s. 6d. had to be deducted for the claims of preferential creditors payable in full, leaving net assets at £236 18s. 11d. The Official Receiver reported that the receiving order was made upon the petition of Mr. William Galpin, on December 8th last, the act of bankruptcy being the failure on the part of the debtor to comply with the requirements of a bankruptcy notice served upon him on October 20th, 1894. The debtor states that he commenced business as a builder at 9, Grove Terrace, Church End, Finchley, about eight years ago. He had no capital of his own, but he borrowed about £40 from his wife. He continued to carry on this business at the above address until March, 1894, when he removed to Southend, and he carried on business there until the date of the receiving order. The debtor states that two years ago he was unable to pay his creditors in full, and he employed a person to consult with his creditors, with a view to their accepting an offer of 3s. 6d. in the pound. This person appears to have paid some of the creditors with certain moneys he collected on the debtor's behalf, leaving other creditors unpaid. He states that this person subsequently absconded with about £15 or £20, and he has not heard of him since. He had practically no assets at all, as the scaffolding and stock were included in the hiring agreement made to his wife. He had built two houses for his wife, the money for that purpose being found by the gentleman who owned the land originally. The plots were sold to his wife, and after she had paid 5 per cent. on the purchase price the original owner advanced her £700 by progressive mortgage. The houses cost about £650 to build, but they were not yet complete. He took another contract at Southend, but as he could not get sufficient materials he had to abandon the houses, and they were finished by the vendors. He had never kept better books of account. He used to keep time-sheets at Finchley, but he had not done so at Southend. He had to keep his father, but in return the latter sometimes assisted him with his contracts. He had not repaid the £40 he borrowed of his wife with which to commence business. The two houses belonging to his wife would be worth about £800 when finished. The estate was finally left in the hands of the Official Receiver for summary administration.
GRIFFITHS, JOHN BENJAMIN, builder and timber agent, 4, Charlotte Square, Margate, lately of 32, Grosvenor Place, and the Kent Timber Yard, Margate.—Mr. Worsfold Mowll, Official Receiver for the East Kent district, has issued particulars of this failure. The gross liabilities are £2,737 14s. 4d., of which £666 5s. 3d. is expected to rank against the estate for dividend. The creditors fully secured represent £2,055, and the estimated value of the securities is £4,435 so that there is from this source a surplus of £380 estimated, to go towards the estate. The estimated assets are £687 17s. 3d., showing a surplus on the estate of £21 12s. The debtor attributes his position to want of capital and to having been at great disadvantage and expense. He commenced business in October, 1885, as a builder in Mill Lane, Margate, in partnership with Mr. T. Bartholomew. In January, 1890, he took the Kent Timber Yard at Margate, and from July, 1893, to July, 1894, he was the Margate agent of the Addington Timber Co. In order to carry on the agency, he states, he had to take additional premises, and the remuneration was insufficient to meet the expenses, a loss of upwards of £70 being the result. WHITE, FREDERICK, builder, Landport.—The summary of debtor's statement of affairs discloses liabilities to unsecured creditors, £754 2s. 9d.; assets, £291 1s. 11d.; deficiency, £463 0s. 10d. Alleged causes of failure: "Want of capital, and sickness of children."—The Official Receiver's observations are:—The bankrupt commenced business in Commercial Road, Landport, about thirty years ago in partnership with his father. The partnership was dissolved about six years ago. About August, 1890, under a private arrangement, he paid a composition of 6s. in the pound on liabilities said to amount to £900. The bankrupt states that at this time he was induced to transfer to the trustee under this arrangement the equities of redemption which he valued at £600 for a consideration of £95, or £700. I am asking the trustee for his version of this transaction, which will probably differ from that of the bankrupt. No cash book has been kept, the only books being a day-book and a debtor's ledger. Consequently I am without any information as to the result of the bankrupt's trading or building operations, or as to his financial position at any time during his trading.
