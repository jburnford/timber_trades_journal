FAILURES AND ARRANGEMENTS.
[It must not be overlooked that in many cases creditors whose names are published in these lists hold security for their debts, sometimes for the full amount.]
ANDERSON, WILLIAM, builder, Hastings.—This debtor made an application for his discharge at the Hastings Bankruptcy Court on Monday last. Judge Martineau, having read the report of the Official Receiver, said the bad feature of this case seemed to be that the debtor had given some of his creditors a preference. His Honour eventually suspended the discharge for three years.
BACON, DAVID HENRY, builder, 3, Merton Road, Wimbledon, and Alt Grove, St. George's Road, Wimbledon.—Mr. Alexander Mackintosh, Official Receiver for the Kingston-on-Thames district, on Saturday issued particulars. The gross liabilities are £1,955, of which £480 is expected to rank against the estate for dividend. The assets are returned at £833, showing a surplus of £352. The debtor states that he does not at the present time and to protect the estate, consider himself insolvent. He commenced business at Wimbledon in October, 1892, with a capital of £100, and entered into partnership with a Mr. Humphrys, who had a similar amount. In March, 1893, the partnership was dissolved, the debtor paying his partner £51 9s., and he is still liable to him in the sum of £5. From March, 1893, he carried on business, and in April last entered into an agreement to build four houses at Kingston, the freeholder agreeing to finance him. Some of his creditors then pressed him, and he filed his petition, as he states, to protect the estate. He hopes to bring forward a proposal at the first meeting of creditors on January 11th.
BARRY, DAVID, builder and contractor, Radcliffe-on-Trent.—The statutory meeting of the creditors interested under this failure took place on Friday week at the offices of the Official Receiver, Nottingham. The statement of affairs filed by the debtor showed gross liabilities amounting to £617 6s. 10d., of which £507 11s. 10d. was due to unsecured creditors. The assets were estimated to produce £211 15s. 11d., thus leaving a deficiency of £295 15s. 11d. He attributes his failure to losses on contracts. The estate was left in the hands of the Official Receiver.
