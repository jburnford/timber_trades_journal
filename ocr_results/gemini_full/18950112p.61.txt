Logs from Canada are also suffering from competition of logs from other ports, and also from the quantity of sawn stuff, chiefly from the United States.
Wainscot logs are increasing in favour, and are said to be rapidly displacing the use of American planks and squares.
On birch logs, and ash planks from the States are having a good run, but the ash logs from Canada are neglected.
United States woods are growing in favour, and the staple articles making a good reputation for themselves—not to speak of yellow pine and oak scantlings, though there is a good trade in poplar or canary coloured whitewood log, plank, and boards. These are in good demand, and, indeed, big trade in oak, chiefly plain and quartered, chiefly the latter, was done at in the log, when prime was in strong request, and did well all the year round, while poor wood was neglected, and prices were so so at wretchedly low prices that importers and boards do not make way.
Owing to the narrowness of the market for waney logs, it will be noticed, on reference to our list, that a prejudice to this wood still exists. So far, it has not been a formidable competitor with prime Ohio or Michigan, being sold at one-third less price.
Teak was largely used during the year, and the market is now more favourable than it has been for some time for importers. Competition for Government contracts was very keen, and in one or two cases prices were at a low level, but on the whole were more than ruled in 1893.

DECK CARGO.—THE "BERYL."—William Campbell, master of the steamer "Beryl," lying in Dundee Harbour, was charged at Dundee, before Hon. Sheriff-Substitute Johnstone on December 28th with having brought the "Beryl" to Dundee with a deckload of wood exceeding 3 ft in height on the well deck. He pleaded guilty.
On behalf of the accused, it was stated that the offence was more of a technical offence than otherwise. The cargo was only above 3 ft at one part of the deck. It was 3 ft 4 in. in the after part of the well-deck, but in the after part of the vessel it was less than 3 ft, the average being considerably under 3 ft. The vessel was quite entitled to leave Riga with a deck cargo exceeding 3 ft. The difficulty was in getting into Dundee with it. At the bar the cargo was taken ill, and the vessel was once rather badly stowed. A considerable amount of cargo as bottom weight was not properly arranged. Mr. Campbell hoped justice would be met by a small penalty.
The Fiscal said it was necessary for the protection of crews and vessels that the Act should be enforced, and an example should be made. A fine of £1 was imposed and was paid.

ELECTRIC LIGHTING.
AYR.—The Town Council have just empowered a committee to conclude the purchase of a site for electric light works.
BRECHIN.—The St. Ninian's Square electrical installation grows apace, for the connections are now completed.
DUNDEE.—Within the past month or two there has been so much demand for electric energy from the Dundee Electrical Station in Dudhope Crescent Road, that the officials have been kept very busy. A new engine and dynamo are on order, and will be forwarded by the makers towards the end of January. The new stock is of the same type as is at present working so satisfactorily.
Workemen are engaged laying a main in Seagate, to supply electric energy to the Boase's Brothers, and it is expected that Boase's and Andrew's Buildings will also have the light.
PITTENWEEM.—The engineering works at Ovenstone, of W. Balfour & Son, have just been fitted up with electric light, which seems perfect in every respect. Driven by the motive power used in connection with the works, it is expected to prove very economical.—Electrical Plant.
