ASPEY, SAMUEL D. W., formerly
trading with Alfred Edward Cosgrove, under
the style of Cosgrove & Aspey, joiners and
builders, Salford.—An application was made for
the discharge of the above bankrupt at the Sal-
ford Bankruptcy Court on Monday last, before
his Honour Judge Parry. The Official Receiver reported
that the receiving order was made against the
debtor on April 24th last. The statement of
affairs showed liabilities amounting to £2,180,
and the assets were estimated to realise £75.
His Honour suspended the discharge for two
years.

BEAZER & COLLETT, builders, Old-
field Park, West, Bath.—The public examination
of these debtors was held on Thursday week at
the Bath Bankruptcy Court, before Mr. Regis-
trar Robertson. It appeared from the replies of
the debtor Beazer that that debtor and his partner
commenced business in 1890, with a capital of
£120, chiefly made up of materials. The state-
ment of affairs showed liabilities amounting to
£2,463, and assets £3,800. The money
the property was returned at £3,800, but that
was in the houses. They had cost
they ought to have produced that
reply to Mr. Pearce, who appeared for
the debtors said they owed Mr. Cox
a number. The examination was finally
adjourned until February 21st.

COPE, FREDERICK JOHN JAMES, builder and
timber merchant, Barbourne, Worcester.—Bankrupt
was brought up on Tuesday at the Guildhall, Wor-
cester, for his public examination, before Mr.
Registrar Allen. As his accounts had been only
recently filed, the Official Receiver (Mr. Luke J.
Sharp) was unable to proceed with the examina-
tion. Mr. Braham Barnett, London, on behalf
of the trustee (Mr. J. Millington), however,
asked the debtor a few questions. Debtor
admitted that eighteen months ago he was
being pressed. His book debts, which he
returned at £800 to £1,000, were many of them
statute-barred, and the trustee said that not a
third were good. Debtor now owed his sisters
£2,400. He had betted a little, but had lost
nothing by betting. He denied that betting
was the cause of his failure. The examination
was adjourned until February 19th.

DURRANS, T. H. & SON, 22A, York
York Terrace, Regent's Park, and
Belsize Terrace, N.W.—Under this failure
accounts have been filed showing total liabili-
ties £1,902 3s. 5d., of which £1,188 15s. 7d.
is unsecured, and estimated net assets £283 1s. 9d.
It appears from the statements of the debtors
(Daniel, Arthur, and Frank Durrans) that they
were both employed in their father's business
at 22A, York Terrace, Regent's Park, up to his
death in 1887, and afterwards carried on the
same for their mother (the executrix) until her
death in March, 1892, when the business was
left to them conjointly, and they continued
trading under the old style "T. H. Durrans &
Son," at the same address, also at
Belsize Terrace, and the yard, 4,
Dorset Square, N.W., down to the date of the
receiving order, that on March 29th, 1894,
they took over the business as
solvent, and Frank Durrans brought in
his working capital, which he had
gained of a house he then possessed. They
attribute their failure to depression in trade,
pulls through want of capital.
