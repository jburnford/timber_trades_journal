GRANITE MERCHANTS AND THEIR ORDERS.
—For the past week or two a movement has been in progress in the Aberdeen granite trade which is of considerable importance to manufacturers. On the 14th ult. several of the merchants of raw Swedish granite intimated to the manufacturers a slight increase in the price of the raw material—the rise to take place immediately. Two days afterwards a meeting of the Granite Association took place, at which the general question of the prices of the raw material was fully discussed. Hitherto it has been the practice for a proposed rise to take effect immediately, and the result is frequently awkward, to say the least of it, for the manufacturer who has much as he may have quoted a certain sum for a monument to a customer, and may find the raw material immediately afterwards enhanced in price. As the result of the meeting referred to, a deputation was appointed to confer with granite merchants on the subject, and particularly with a view of having a rule introduced by which one month's notice would be given of the proposed alteration in the price of the raw material. The deputation of the merchants heartily met by the merchants, who at once agreed to quote the old prices for current contracts, and within the last day or two they, and practically all the principal merchants, have agreed to the proposed rule.

PERTH MUNICIPAL BUILDINGS.—The municipal buildings, Perth, of which the greatest part, with the valuable contents, has been destroyed by fire, were built about fifteen years ago from the designs of Messrs. A. & A. Heiton, of that city. The elevations were faced with freestone from Bonfield Quarry, in Fife, the internal stonework being from the local Huntingtower Quarries. Erected, at a cost of about £11,000, on the site of the old Council Chambers and some adjoining property acquired by the Police Commissioners, and having frontages to Tay and High Streets, the block comprised offices for the Police, the Registrar, and the City Chamberlain on the ground floor; and on the first floor a council-chamber, 41 ft. by 25 ft., a burgh court-room, 30 ft. by 22 ft., and the Burgh-Assessor's office. It is stated that the flames broke out in a overheated flue, and that some portraits and busts, with all the town maps and plans, have been consumed. Perth received a municipal constitution forty years ago, and was made a city in 1880.

ALUMINIUM.—At the Glasgow Philosophical Society, Professor A. H. Sexton, Glasgow and West of Scotland Technical College, read a paper on "Aluminium." Aluminium, he said, might be regarded as a very valuable addition to our meagre list of useful metals, and one that which increasing use would certainly not be made as time goes on; aluminium would supersede iron and steel for constructive purposes on a large scale. It would, however, be largely used for lighter articles where lightness or resistance to corrosion was so important, and would be valuable. It was likely to be very largely used for domestic purposes. It was a silver, little, if any, dearer than nickel or German silver, and, therefore, it was likely to be much used for ornamental purposes. There was no doubt that the use of the metal had a very promising future.

MASTER BUILDERS MEET.
The twenty-third annual meeting of the and Leith Master Builders' Association was held at 5, St. Andrew Square, Edinburgh. Mr. David Heron, president, occupied the chair. The secretary's report showed a membership of 97 employers. The principal business before the meeting was the question of an eight hours day. Reports were submitted of meetings held with the masons and joiners respectively, and it was agreed, with respect to the masons, that a deputation be appointed to meet the men, on the question, if possible, by arbitration. The employer members of the Joiners' Board were appointed for the ensuing year, and it was agreed to urge the joiners to definitely decide as to whether they could accept the offer of the employers of a maximum week of forty-eight hours, and if not, that the Conciliation Board take up the question.

NEW CHAPEL AT EDINBURGH.—A new Free Presbyterian chapel is about to be built at the corner of Slateford Road and Shandon Crescent, Edinburgh, at a cost of about £5,000. The building will be in the Scottish Fifteenth Century style, will measure 890 ft. by 61 ft., and will accommodate 760 persons. Messrs. M'Carthy & Watson, Frederick Street, Edinburgh, are the architects.
