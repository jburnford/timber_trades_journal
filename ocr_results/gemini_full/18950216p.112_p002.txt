BARRY, DAVID, contractor, Radcliffe-on-Trent.—This debtor was examined at the Nottingham Bankruptcy Court on Friday last, before the Registrar. The summary of accounts filed by the debtor disclosed liabilities amounting to £507 10s. 6d., estimated to produce £211. BRAID, A., builder, 112 and 113, Fore Street, E.C.—In this case, the Official Receiver refused to approve the debtor's proposal to pay his creditors a composition of 7s. 6d. in the pound. CLARKE, WALTER, builder, 88, Glen Road, Abbeydale Road, Sheffield.—The adjourned public examination of this debtor was held at the Sheffield Bankruptcy Court, on Thursday week, before his Honour Judge Ellison. The statement of affairs filed by the debtor disclosed gross liabilities amounting to £2,277, and the assets were estimated to produce £3,176. The debtor was examined at considerable length by Mr. Booth, of Halifax, who appeared for the trustee, on several matters connected with the private examination of several witnesses. The examination was ordered to be closed.
CLAYTON, THOMAS, joiner and builder, Mount Pellon, Halifax.—The statutory meeting of the creditors interested under this failure was held on Saturday last at the offices of the Official Receiver, Halifax. The statement of affairs filed by the debtor showed liabilities amounting to £306 19s. 5d. It appeared that the debtor commenced business 34 years ago with a capital of £240. His deficiency had been caused through bad trade and losses on contracts during the past five years. Eventually the estate was left in the hands of the Official Receiver for summary administration.

TRADE NOTES.
IT is admitted on all hands that the trade during the week has not been up to expectation, and only a few fortunate firms are able to report no falling off in their turnover as compared with the corresponding week of last year. It says much for the stability of the trade and the stability of the district that quotations are maintained and a decidedly hopeful feeling prevails even in face of the severe check to all immediate deliveries caused by the almost Arctic weather experienced without a break for the last six weeks.
Not only has the building trade been checked from the first, but latterly shipbuilding and every other form of outdoor labour is used has been almost at a standstill. While this is true of Scotland, it is specially felt in Glasgow and neighbourhood.
The untoward situation has this compensation, however, in prospect, that with a reopening of navigation the present congested state of work shortly expected will get rapidly reduced, and in this way the trade may find itself short supplied.
Pitch offers are at firm rates, and very little is offering. Freights are to close important contracts. Freights are low, but not appreciably lower than last season nor likely to decline.
