TRADE REPORTS.
GLASGOW.
The severe weather, which for the first two
months of the year has very much restricted
business, is at last giving place to milder con-
ditions, allowing of outdoor work proceeding
and improving the tone of business
generally.
In connection with shipbuilding it is esti-
mated that at one time were fully 15,000 men
idle on the Clyde owing to the intense
frost which prevailed. In housebuilding the
stoppage has been of long duration, and
things have been much kept back, so that the
period between this and the term will be a
busy one if lost time is to be made up.
There has been no auction sale to note here
since last writing, and in the way of
deliveries the movement has been only
moderate, but no doubt will improve now
with the advent of milder weather. A public
sale is announced for next Wednesday by
Messrs. Singleton, Dunn, & Co., at which
various shipments of deals, &c., are to be
offered, and the result will afford a test of
the tendency of prices in this market as
regards the goods indicated. From recent
transactions so far as transpired there appears
no weakening of prices, and the position of
stocks and general outlook point rather to
the probability of a rise, especially in certain
descriptions.

GREENOCK.
No arrivals to report this week; trade is
very quiet, but the outlook is more cheering.
Two new contracts have been obtained this
week by Messrs. Russell & Co., one for three-
masted sailing ship of 2,600 tons for Messrs.
R. Shankland & Co., Greenock, and one for a
four-masted schooner for Messrs. A. Russell,
of Glasgow.
The only launch here this month was the
steamer "Kalgau," of 2,000 tons and 1,500
h.-p., built and engined by Messrs. Scott &
Co. for the China Navigation Company.
At Port Glasgow the tug "Flying
Bussard" was the only vessel launched during
the month. Her builders are Messrs. Robt.
Duncan & Co., Limited.

LEITH.
The only arrivals of wood goods to report
this week are 1,759 pices oak and 34 logs
poplar per "Montana" (s), from Baltimore,
but some shipments are expected shortly
from Drontheim and other Norwegian ports.
There has been a rise in temperature,
although not quite of such a decided
character as could be wished, and outdoor
work is being resumed with perceptible
benefit to every industry directly or indirectly
concerned. The wood trade is one of the first
to feel the advantage of the change, and the
deliveries from the docks and yards have
been larger this week than for some time.
There has been no public sale this week, but
several are announced.

ELECTRICITY FROM WATER-POWER.—A
great amount of public interest has been
roused in the north of Scotland in connection
with the formation of a British aluminium
company to utilise the water-power of the
Falls of Foyers, on Loch Ness, which form
one of the great attractions for tourists
during the summer. It is proposed to bring
the material from Ireland to Foyers, and
there manufacture the metal with electricity
by motive power. The works will give
employment to 100 men in a district where
at present there is little or no population;
the buildings, the company state, will
be of an ornamental character to harmonise
with the beauty of the surroundings. Public
feeling was somewhat allayed by an official
statement made at a meeting on Friday to
the effect that it is not proposed to do away
with the falls, and that the flow of water will
be affected in very dry seasons. As
tourists from all parts of the world visit the
Falls, the subject is one of national interest,
and it is understood that the Secretary for
Scotland has ordered an official report on the
proposed operations of the company.
NORTH OF SCOTLAND HORTICULTURAL
AND ARBORICULTURAL ASSOCIATION.—The
monthly meeting of this association was
held in the Upper Hall of the Christian
Institute, Aberdeen, last week, Mr. Mor-
grow presiding. A paper on "How Plants
Grow" was to have been read by Mr. G.
Tough, but owing to the fact that only a
few members were present it was
decided that Mr. Tough should read his paper
at the next monthly meeting. The Secretary
thereafter read a few meteorological notes on
the weather of 1894 and its effects on vege-
tation, which had been sent by Mr. Harper,
of Archie Park. In these notes Mr. Harper
pointed out that the season that has just
passed must be classed as an unfavourable one,
one benefit which would, however, accrue
from the severe frosts would be the reduction
in the number of vermin, of which they had
previously had such an abundance. The
paper also submitted notes of the climatic
conditions in the various months during the
year, mentioning the direction of the wind
and the average rainfall during the twelve
months, it being shown that the average
rainfall for the whole year was 29 in. He
also made special reference to the
abnormal wind-storms of the year and to the
various phenomena which had been observed
such as the waves of heat which were felt
in Aberdeen on Christmas Day. Mr. Harper
was accorded a vote of thanks for his
interesting statement.

STRANRAER TRADE.
1894, if not a record year, has
been a good deal better than some of
the past years. Masons' work in
the district have been well employed
the year, and there are no idle hands
so far.
Three large and fine buildings are
at present in the line of erection in their
respective ways—the Academy, which is the
finest buildings in the town, and Mr.
A. Hannay's extensive coachbuilding works,
and the Parish Church of Inch, which is the
comeliest in the county, having been destroyed
by fire.
The massive and strong structure of
the timber pond of the Stranraer
Saw-mills (Messrs. J. A. A. Wallace) was
levelled by the high tide and storm of
December 22nd.
A great many farm buildings were
unroofed by the same storm, which for
a time caused an extra press of work and
trade, and as the expense of repairing the
ravages of the storm will be heavy,
this may to a certain extent affect the
carrying out of new buildings and improve-
ments, and we anticipate that it will affect the
trade to a very large extent.
