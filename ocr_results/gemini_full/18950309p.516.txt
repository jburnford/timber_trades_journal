TRADE REPORTS.
GLASGOW.
The arrivals are, of course, just now very limited, and a glance at the list will show that the only items for the past week are a few small parcels United States lumber and staves.

Of walnut and whitewood logs there has been a very restricted import this year so far compared with the same time last year, and prices naturally are strong. The bulk of the walnut logs in stock are of medium to large small-sized. Parcels of whitewood logs (Canary) find ready sale on arrival. LEITH.
Since last report the "Tronda" (s) has arrived from Drontheim with 7,891 deals and battens, but so far there are no further importations of wood goods.

The goods offered consisted chiefly of last season's imports, but the above fresh shipment from Drontheim was also included in the catalogue. There was an average attendance of buyers, but the demand was not active, and although a fair proportion of the various parcels offered was disposed of, there was nothing like a clearance effected. NENTHORN.—Extensive improvements have just been completed on the estate of Nenthorn, on the border-line of Roxburghshire and Berwickshire. A new mansion-house has been built (the architects being Messrs. Beattie & Sons, Edinburgh), and electric lighting and a new water supply have been introduced.

KEMNAY QUARRIES.—Great preparations are in progress for the further development of the Kemnay Quarries which have been carried on in the past by the owner, Mr. Fyfe. The quarries already opened are worked on the most advanced principle, and the new quarry will be carried on on the same system. There is to be a powerful steam crane placed in it, and a line of rails laid down to form a direct connection with the Alford Valley Railway.

MARCH 9, 1895.
OBITUARY.
MR. ALEXANDER IMLAY.
Many of our readers will learn with much regret of the death of Mr. Alexander Imlay, of the Fairmuir Saw-mills and Timber Works, Dundee, which took place on Thursday, the 28th ult.
The deceased gentleman was 68 years of age, and was one of the oldest timber merchants in his district, having commenced business in Queen Street, Dundee, so far ago as 1855. The extensive works at Fairmuir, in which the business is now carried on, were erected to meet the increasing requirements of the trade.
About a year ago the late Mr. Imlay retired from active participation in the business, but until quite recent he continued to maintain an active interest in the concern with which his name was so honourably identified. He was deservedly well known in the trade, and his genial presence will be much missed by those who were wont to come in contact with him.
The business has long been under the direction of Mr. W. G. Imlay, who, with the remaining partners, will continue to trade in the same style as heretofore.
MR. SAMUEL MORRIS.
The trade in Scotland will regret to learn of the death of Mr. Samuel Morris, who had attained the ripe age of 73, and for the past forty years conducted a large and well-known business in Glasgow, as agent for several firms. We understand he has been in failing health for some months past. His demise took place at Bournemouth on the 4th inst., where he had gone in hope to recruit.

PERTH.—Referring to the report on the Perth given in our Special Issue, a correspondent sends a correction of the timber returns there given. These should have read as follows:—Hewn timber, 889 loads; sawn timber, 5,920 loads; making a total of 6,809 loads for the year. These were all received direct into Perth.

EDINBURGH.—The Public Health Committee of the Edinburgh Town Council have resolved that temporary accommodation be provided, by the erection of wooden or iron huts in the quadrangles of the fever hospital for the nurses. The special sub-committee appointed to consider the establishment of branch libraries in connection with the public library reported in favour of erecting branch libraries in three districts. Subsequently, at a joint meeting of the Nelson Trustees and the sub-committee, it was agreed to a general understanding as to the sites they came to.
