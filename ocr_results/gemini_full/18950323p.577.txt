PETERHEAD. — The Town Council have approved a scheme for the erection of a model lodging-house at a cost of about £1,200.
BRICK v. GRANITE.—At the Aberdeen Trades Council meeting Mr. John Anderson, mason, said he wished to refer to the decision of the Town Council to erect a brick building at the bathing station. The Masons' Society thought this was scarcely the thing for the Town Council to do in the Granite City. Bricks were good enough in their place, but they should be kept in their place. A brick stalk for the infirmary was objected to by some members of the present Town Council on account of its appearance. What then could they expect of a brick building stand-ing in an isolated position? He moved that the matter be remitted to the Vigilance Committee. Mr. Wisely, joiner, moved that the Council protest against the finding of the Town Council on this matter, and this was incorporated with Mr. Anderson's motion. The motion, with the addition by Mr. Wisely, was unanimously carried.
SOCIETY OF ANTIQUARIES OF SCOTLAND.
The usual monthly meeting of the Society of Antiquaries of Scotland has been held, the Hon. Hew Dalrymple in the chair. The first paper, on "The Monumental Effigies of Scotland from Thirteenth to Fifteenth Century," by Mr. Robert Brydall, F.S.A. Scot., St. George's Art School, Glasgow, was illustrated by a series of drawings by the author. The custom of carving monumental effigies in full relief, he said, did not appear to have prevailed in Scotland until the thirteenth century, the incised slab, of peculiar treatment of the figure in flat relief, having been the previous monumental forms. While it was on record that certain tombs or effigies such as those of the Bruce and Robert II. were sculptured on the Continent and in England, the greatest number were executed in Scotland. The destruction and dilapidation of these splendid works of art in Scotland was scarcely conceivable until the list was reckoned up. They excepted the mutilated coffin lid of William the Lion at Arbroath, the effigy known as Marjory Bruce, at Paisley, and the unidentified Stewart of Bute, at Rothesay, they had no royal effigies left in Scotland. Of those of church dignitaries, most had disappeared, while of those that remained none had escaped mutilation or decay. Even the knightly effigies, of which more examples had been preserved, were for the most part in much the same sad state. In strong contrast to the English effigies, with their beautifully enamelled heraldry and metal figures, the Scottish effigies, with the exception of two or three in marble, were of ordinary stone, and the only suggestion of the use of other material was in the hollows for the face and hands in the fine incised slab at Creich, also in contrast with England. There was no doubt that many of the Scottish effigied tombs were coloured and gilt; and worn, defaced, and mutilated as they were, their study was still full of interest to the artist, the antiquary, and the historian. KIRKCALDY.—A new road is to be made by the Town Council at a cost of £1,000.
CRIEFF.—The erection of a hospital for infectious diseases at a cost of £1,000 is proposed by the Town Council.
NEW ROAD DEPOT AT TYNECASTLE.— The Streets and Buildings Committee of the Edinburgh Town Council have resolved to recommend the acceptance of an estimate of £1,700 for the erection of a new road depot and offices at Tynecastle.
SHIPBUILDING ORDERS.—It is reported that Messrs. Chas. Connell & Co., ship-builders, Scotstoun, have received an order for a steamer of 6,000 tons deadweight, for Dundee owners. Messrs. Rowan & Co., Glasgow, are to supply the engines. Messrs Gourlay Brothers & Co., Dundee, have received an order to build a very fast twin screw steamer for owners abroad.
THE EDINBURGH ARCHITECTURAL ASSOCIATION.—The members of this association recently paid a visit to the New Royal Observatory, on Blackford Hill, where the president, who is also the architect of the building, gave them a short description of the principal features of the new buildings, which include the Observatory proper, the Astronomer Royal's house, houses for the assistants, and a lodge for the accommodation.
