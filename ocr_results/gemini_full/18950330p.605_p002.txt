FAILURES AND ARRANGEMENTS.
[It must not be overlooked that in many cases creditors whose names are published in these lists hold security for their debts, sometimes for the full amount.]
BALL, THOMAS, senr., builder, of Chudleigh.—At the office of the Official Receiver, Exeter, a first meeting of the creditors of this debtor was held yesterday week. Mr. C. T. K. Roberts appeared for debtor. The liabilities to unsecured creditors amounted to £238 17s., and the assets were returned at £31 9s., leaving a deficiency of £207 16s. 3d. He attributed his failure to losses on building contracts, losses through ill-health, and expenditure on mortgaged property. Debtor filed his petition in consequence of an execution having been levied on his effects. He commenced business in 1879 with £200 borrowed capital. He became aware of his insolvency twelve months ago, and admitted that since then he had contracted debts, but he thought he should get back something of what he had laid out on his mortgaged property. The Official Receiver remarked that it was to be regretted that people like the debtor did not face their creditors earlier, whilst there was something to divide, instead of waiting until their money was all gone, or nearly so. The usual resolution was adopted.
BICKLEY, ALBERT EDWARD, builder, of Earl's Court, Sefton Park, Ashley Hill.—Mr. F. Spofforth was for the debtor and Mr. C. E. Barry for the creditors. At the Bristol Bankruptcy Court, last week, the Official Receiver examined the debtor, who said his wife claimed the furniture. He was originally a bricklayer, and after marriage, his wife having means, he started as a builder. After questioning the debtor as to his financial relations with his wife, the Official Receiver said it appeared that money that was coming in belonged to the wife, and the debts owing belonged to the husband (laughter). He had no money of his own when he began building; he was actually in debt. The examination was adjourned to May 10th.

FARRAND, JOE SYKES, joiner and builder, Huddersfield.—A meeting of creditors was held at the offices of Messrs. Armitage, Sykes, & Hinchcliffe, solicitors, 13, Westgate, Huddersfield, on March 19th. It was attended by nearly the whole of the creditors. After considerable discussion, and an examination of the debtor, the creditors present unanimously resolved that the estate be left in the hands of three of the largest trade creditors—viz., Messrs. Jere Kaye & Co., timber merchants, Huddersfield; Messrs. Sanderson Bros., Paddock, Huddersfield; and Messrs. J. Robinson & Sons, Marsh, Huddersfield, to be dealt with as they may think advisable in the interests of the creditors. In the opinion of the meeting the assets of the debtor were not likely to realise, in the ordinary course, anything near the amount estimated in the statement. The view of the meeting was that the debtor should pay a sum of 7s. 6d. in the pound, but he expressed himself unable to do this at present. The resolution above referred to was, therefore, decided upon.

MACKAY, FRANCIS, builder and contractor, Preston, North Shields, at the Newcastle Bankruptcy Court, yesterday week, before Mr. Lloyd, Deputy Judge, applied for his discharge. Mr. Bramwell appeared for the debtor, whose debts, he said, amounting to £129, arose through one unfortunate building transaction. The friends of the debtor proposed to pay the sum of £50 into court, and he asked his Honour to grant the discharge on those conditions. The Official Receiver did not oppose the application, and Mr. C. J. Brown, who appeared for creditors representing eight-tenths of the debt, was also agreeable to the discharge being granted on the terms proposed. The discharge was granted subject to the payment of the £50.
