DAYLISS, HENRY, timber merchant, carrying on business at 83, Kensal Road, and residing at 22, Abbey Street, Bethnal Green, and Kensal Road, and at 178, Kilburne Park. This failure occurred in October 1894, and the bankrupt applied on Monday to Mr. Registrar Giffard for his order of discharge. The accounts showed unsecured liabilities £1,115 4s. 10d. and assets nil. Proofs amounting to £715 16s. 6d. have been lodged, and it is reported that the assets have realised £322 14s. 6d. and that a dividend of about 2s. 6d. in the pound will be paid. It appeared that the bankrupt commenced as a timber merchant without capital of his own, and traded at Abbey Street, Bethnal Green, under the style of Bayliss & Co. In February, 1888, he took additional premises at 83, Kensal Road, and purchased the stock of timber there from the former proprietor for £600, in respect of which £75 is still owing. He attributes his failure to the seizure, in November, 1893, of stock at Abbey Street and Kensal Road, by the holders of a bill of sale given by the bank-

COPE, J. C., timber merchant, Derby.-The adjourned public examination of this bankrupt took place on Tuesday at the Derby Bankruptcy Court. The bankrupt was questioned at considerable length with regard to his connection with the firm of Matthews, of London, of which he was a partner, and said that Matthews & Co. were now £2,000 to the worse off in regard to their bill transactions with them, and that all the bills he had discounted for them was in respect of goods supplied. The examination was then adjourned.
