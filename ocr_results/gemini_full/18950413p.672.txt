COLLIESTON HARBOUR.—For the execution of the harbour works at Collieston, the offer of Mr. Alexander Forbes, Ellon, has been accepted by the Harbour Trustees.
The extension of the harbour will now be proceeded with without delay.

MESSRS. C. DAVIDSON & SONS, LIMITED, Auchmill, near Aberdeen, have placed upon the market a patent anti-damp foundation fall specially made in various widths to suit walls of different thicknesses.
It is absolutely watertight, and being pliable is not liable to crack if the wall subsides.
It is easily applied, and effectually prevents any damp from ascending the wall.

HAMILTON.—A special meeting of the Town Council of Hamilton was held on Thursday evening in last week, to consider the question of the extension of the gasworks.
The Gas Committee recommended extensions at an estimated cost of £18,740.
The scheme was adopted under modifications, the effect of reducing the total cost by about £3,000.

THE DUNDEE INSTITUTE OF ARCHITECTURE, SCIENCE, AND ART.—The Institute of Architects' prize drawings will be exhibited in the Permanent Gallery of the Albert Institute (entrance by Museum door) until Saturday, the 13th.
The attention of all architects, assistants, and pupils is particularly requested to these drawings.
By the alliance with the R.I.B.A. the Institute obtains the favour of this exhibition, and it is only courteous that it should be visited, as without such alliance the drawings could not be seen outside of London, and an inspection of them will be of great benefit to every member or pupil as well as master of the architectural profession in the district.

STEAMSHIP COMMUNICATION BETWEEN DUNDEE AND NORWAY.—The Faerder Steam-ship Company, Limited, of Christiania, have arranged to place one of their steamers on the passage between Dundee and Christiania.
Mr. P. S. Nicol, Dock Street, Dundee, has been appointed agent for the new line, and the first vessel to leave Dundee for Norway direct will be the "Faerder" (s), on or about the 26th inst.
Thereafter there will be fortnightly communication between Dundee and the Norwegian capital.
The Faerder Company own 17 steamers, including the Norwegian Royal mail steamships between Grangemouth and Christiania.

PRESBYTERIAN CHURCH, HAMILTON, LANARKSHIRE.—The memorial stone has been laid of Avon Street United Presbyterian Church, Hamilton.
The new structure is to replace the present church, which was erected in 1779.
The church has been erected from plans by Mr. J. B. Wilson, architect, Glasgow.
The contractors are:—Mason, R. Downie, Hamilton; wright, J. C. Burns & Co., Larkhall; slater and plasterer, James Currie, Hamilton; plumber, W. P. Mitchell; painter, S. Kemp, jun., Hamilton; glaziers, McCulloch & Co.; wrought iron, M'Callum & Hope, Glasgow; heating, Boyd & Sons, Paisley.

PERTH.—A special meeting of the Perth Established Presbytery was held to consider as to the restoration of St. John's Church, Perth.
A proposal was made to the Pres-bytery to sanction the taking down of the division wall between the east and middle churches.
Mr. Grainger Heiton, architect, produced a plan showing the building re-stored to cost £2,265, with sitting accommo-dation for 1,050.
Mr. Heiton explained that the main feature of the restoration scheme was the abolition of the division wall, which was erected in 1771.
The edifices comprising the west, middle, and east churches were erected by the Court of Session in 1807.
It was proposed to restore the pulpit to its old position in the north-east transept, while the organ at present in the east church would be removed to near the north transept.
The five fine arches at present built up would be opened out, and the proportions of the church would be then seen to advantage.
The sittings in the east church would be lessened by 69.
The Town Council have decided unanimously in favour of the restoration scheme, but by only a narrow majority of the presbytery, 13 to 11, it was decided at a recent meeting to give approval to the restoration scheme.

SAW-MILLS IN GREENOCK.—The plot of ground in Macdougall Street, with saw-mills, offices, fixed machinery, boat-building yard, and cottage, lately occupied by Messrs Allan Cameron & Son, were exposed for sale on a recent afternoon within the Fontine Hotel.
The upset price was £900, where it was sold to a Glasgow agent for a client.
Messrs. W. W. and J. M'Clure, solicitors, were agents in the sale.

THE DUNDEE CATTLE TRADE.—In accordance with the request of the Dundee Harbour Trust, the Board of Agriculture have revoked the order for landing cattle at the Canadian cattle depot, which enables the Trustees to use the wharves for the purpose of general trade.
It has been found that the Trust are unable to wholly suspend dredging operations, and the Works Committee have resolved to recommend that £800 be spent in order to keep the eastern wharves in such a state as to accommodate vessels of the largest draught.

COUNTY OF ABERDEEN.—Mr. J. D. Watson, son, engineer to the County Council of Aberdeen, has issued his report for the year 1894.
Water and drainage schemes, involving preliminary surveys, preparations of plans and specifications, and superintendence of works, were carried out in eight localities, while preliminary plans and estimates were presented to the Council in connection with nine other schemes.
Much work had to be done in effecting alterations and improvements at a rather large number of police stations.
A great undertaking was the surveying of the Don Valley for a distance of five miles, and preparation of plans showing the proposed route of the new sewer calculated to carry to the sea outfall 31,000,000 gallons of sewage per day.
This sewer would obviate the pollution of the river.
Heavy work was also thrown on the engineer by the coming into force of the Local Government (Scotland) Act 1894, which necessitated plans showing the division of parishes into wards.
