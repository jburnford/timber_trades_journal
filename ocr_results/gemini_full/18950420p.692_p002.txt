FENN, WILLIAM, builder, Bridge Street, Northampton.—This debtor was publicly examined in the Northampton Bankruptcy Court on the 11th inst., before Mr. Registrar Faulkner.
In answer to the Official Receiver he said he formerly carried on business in John Street, Northampton.
His deficiency amounted to £277 2s. 9d.
He had failed on a previous occasion, in 1886, when his liabilities amounted to £1,121 6s 8d., and he paid his creditors a dividend of 7s. 6d. in the pound.
He commenced business in the first place about seventeen years ago.
He was then in difficulties in 1888, when he paid a composition of 3s. 6d. in the pound.
From 1888 to 1892 he managed the business for his wife.
He then commenced on his own account again.
During the last three years he had been in the County Court continually paying through the nose for his goods.
He first became aware of his insolvent position about two years ago.
The examination was eventually ordered to be adjourned.

JONES & PREECE, builders, late of Smeaton Street, Cardiff.—An application was made for the discharge of the above debtors at the last sitting of the Cardiff Bankruptcy Court before his Honour Judge Owen.
It appeared that the receiving order was made on June 7th, 1893, on the debtors' own petition, and the Official Receiver reported that the major portion of the assets returned by the debtors appeared on paper only.
He alleged that the bankruptcy had been caused through rash and hazardous speculation, and that the bankrupts had carried on their business in a very reckless manner.
Eventually his Honour refused to grant the discharge.

MORGAN BROS., builders, Cadoxton, Barry Dock and Cardiff.—The statement of affairs and report and observations of the Official Receiver have now been issued under this failure, from which it appears that the gross liabilities amount to £4,157 18s. 1d., of which £1,200 11s. 7d. is expected to rank against the estate for dividend.
The assets are estimated to produce £556 7s., thus leaving a deficiency of £644 4s. 7d.
The debtors allege their failure to have been caused through "depression in trade in the Barry district, inability to let the property, heavy bankers' charges, and illness."
The report and observations of the Official Receiver are to the following effect:—"The receiving order was made upon the debtors' own petition, and they have intimated that they are unable to submit any offer of composition to their creditors.
They commenced business at Cadoxton in November, 1883, with a capital of £70, and opened a branch shop at Quay Street, Cardiff, about two years ago, hoping to extend their business to the Cardiff district, as in consequence of depression of trade in and about Cadoxton their trade had fallen away.
They have only kept debtors' ledgers and day books, which do not disclose their financial position at any time or show the result of their trading.
They estimated that the business was worked at a profit down to fifteen months ago, but since then it has made a considerable loss.
They have drawn from the business for household and personal expenses about 35s. per week.
In addition to their ordinary trade transactions they have erected various properties in Barry and Cadoxton and a house and shop at Barry.
The Cadoxton properties were mortgaged to the fully secured creditors, and the partly secured creditors also have a charge upon them, together with a first mortgage on the shop at Barry.
This shop has been unoccupied for the last two years, and this is partly the cause of the debtors' failure.
The debtors estimate the value of these properties to be the same as the charges upon them.
The household furniture at present used by the bankrupts is claimed by their respective wives, as having been purchased out of their separate estates and acquired by gifts from their parents.
The furniture belonging to the bankrupts was sold by them in August, 1894, and in February last, the latter sale being for the purpose of obtaining money to pay wages and to pay out an execution.
The figures accounting for the deficiency are mere estimates."
The first meeting of the creditors interested hereunder took place on the 11th inst. at the offices of the Official Receiver, Cardiff.
The Official Receiver, who presided, after reading the proofs, said the business before the meeting was to appoint a trustee and committee of inspection.
After some discussion it was resolved to appoint Mr. C. E. Dovey chartered accountant, as trustee to act in conjunction with a committee of inspection.

MUNN, HENRY, builder and contractor, Windsor Approach, Waterloo Road, S.E. This failure occurred in September, 1894, and the bankrupt applied last week to Mr. Registrar Brougham for his order of discharge.
It appeared that proofs amounting to £1,039 11s. 10d. had been made by thirty creditors, and that the assets, which were estimated at £233 had realised £158 9s. 10d., and that a further sum of £20 might be received.
It was reported that the assets were not equal to 10s. in the pound, that the bankrupt had not kept proper books of accounts, and that he had kept on trading after knowledge of insolvency.
His Honour on these grounds suspended the discharge for two years.
