BERNAS, ALBERT, Love Lane, Oldswinford, builder.-This debtor came up for his public examination on Wednesday at the Court House, Stourbridge, before Mr. Registrar Collis. His unsecured liabilities were £354 8s. 4d., and assets £44 10s. 3d., leaving a deficiency of £309 18s. 2d. His examination by Mr. Jobson (the Official Receiver) showed that he formerly carried on business at Bath, where he paid a composition of 10s. in the pound. He went to Stourbridge about three years ago, and he had since carried on business there as a builder. He had contracted part of his present liabilities at Stourbridge, and part were liabilities at Bath. He had been paying nothing but costs for the last twelve months. The examination was closed.
BURDEN, ROBERT, timber merchant, of Chester and Gwersyllt, near Wrexham.-The statement of affairs has been issued in this bankruptcy. The gross liabilities amount to £69,937 19s. 6d. (expected to rank £60,399 15s. 8d.), and the assets are estimated at £2,774 0s. 10d., leaving a deficiency of £57,625 14s. 10d. The unsecured creditors are scheduled at £35,820 5s. 8d. The debtor states his cause of failure to be "chiefly losses on accommodation bills, trade losses (especially during the last twelve months), overstocking in 1892 and 1893, depreciation in value of stock-in-trade, and heavy interest to money-lenders." The Official Receiver, in his observations on the case, after referring to the debtor's imperfect system of bookkeeping, says: "Previous to March, 1891, the bankrupt had been in the employ of Messrs. Barker Brothers, timber merchants, Shrewsbury. In that month he started in business on his own account with a capital of £1,000, which was given him by his aunt, Mrs. Burch, of Lynn, and £2,000 he obtained upon her guarantee from the Burton Union Bank. The bankrupt appears in the years 1892 and 1893 to have made very large purchases of timber in all parts of the country, receiving financial assistance for the purpose chiefly from Messrs. Thrutchley Brothers, of Knutsford. The coal strike undoubtedly stopped his getting off his heavy stocks, and materially contributed to increase the financial difficulties which he had got himself into by his purchases so much beyond his means. But apart from this I think the bankrupt's statement to me that some of his purchases were wild in the extreme is accurate, and that he disposed of a large quantity of his stock at much less than it had cost him. In May of last year he commenced to raise money by means of mutual

accommodation bills which he obtained from various parties, to a large number of whom he was introduced by a firm, Messrs. Bleichroeder and Co., financial agents, London. The arrangement with these parties was that the bankrupt was to discount the bills, remit half the proceeds to the acceptor forthwith, and the balance just before the bills fell due. This, however, was not carried out, and in some cases the bankrupt exchanged the acceptances of other people, and in a few cases gave his own acceptances in exchange. The bills received by the bankrupt and thus dealt with since last May numbered at least 150, and amounted in the aggregate to over £31,000, and this does not include bills accepted for the bankrupt's sole accommodation. Of bankrupt's unsecured liabilities, only about £6,000 represents trade debts, the remainder represents his liabilities to creditors for cash and accommodation. During the latter half of 1894 bankrupt increased his insolvency by borrowing large sums from money-lenders at ruinous rates of interest. The bankrupt states that he has been aware of his insolvency for twelve months. On November 9th last he gave notice to all the stationmasters at stations where he had timber lying, stating that he had purchased the timber as agent for Messrs. Thrutchley Brothers, Limited, and requesting them to hold it at the latter's disposal. There is a doubt as to the validity of this transaction. The bankrupt's timber yard at Gwersyllt is leasehold for fourteen years at a rent of £50 per annum with option of purchase at the price of £1,200. He states that he has spent over £1,200 upon the place in the erection of offices and buildings and for machinery. The lease is mortgaged to a creditor as security for a debt of £1,105." Since writing these observations the Official Receiver has received the proof of Messrs. Thrutchley Brothers, Limited, showing a balance due to them of £20,920 17s. 3d., which exceeds the bankrupt's estimate by £8,300.
SULLIVANT, ARTHUR, builder, Headingley, Leeds.-The statutory meeting of the creditors interested under this failure took place at the offices of the Official Receiver, Leeds, on Monday last, but as there was not a quorum of creditors present no resolutions could be passed. The estate was left in the hands of the Official Receiver for summary administration.

DUCKETT, GEORGE, builder, 72, Esson Road, Ramsgate.-This debtor, appearing for his adjourned examination at Norwich Bankruptcy Court on Wednesday, returned his liabilities at £2307 10s. 3d., assets £102 5s. 6d., deficiency £2205 3s. 11d. The examination had been adjourned from the last Court for a statement to be prepared of debtor's receipts and payments. This was now presented, and Mr. Back, who appeared for the petitioning creditor, offering no objection to the accounts, the examination was closed.
