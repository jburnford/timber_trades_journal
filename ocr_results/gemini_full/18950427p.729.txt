OBITUARY.—The death is announced of Mr. George Thompson, of Pitmedden, Aberdeenshire, the founder of the well-known Aberdeen Line, trading with Australia. The deceased gentleman, who was 91 years of age, was amongst the most prominent citizens of Aberdeen, which city he at one time represented in Parliament. He was a liberal contributor to the philanthropic and charitable institutions of the city, in which he took a warm interest. He commenced business in Aberdeen as a shipowner, insurance broker, and a timber importer, and gradually acquired a large business, with Australia. He started a line of traders to the colony, and his vessels were amongst the first to make regular trips between the two countries. In 1850 Mr. Thompson was joined in partnership by his son-in-law, Sir William Henderson. A London house was opened four years later, and the partnership has been extended by the admission of Mr. Thompson's sons. Mr. Thompson had for many years retired from active business.

A PARK FOR AIRDRIE.—At the meeting of the Airdrie Town Council it was announced that Mr. Alexander, of Airdrie House, offered to present the town with the Mill Field as a public park for the town. The ground extends to nearly eight acres, and is in a central position.

THE TELEPHONE AT EDINBURGH.—Edinburgh Town Council contemplate applying to the Board of Trade for a licence authorising them to establish telephonic communication within the city. It is said that with a double service of wires great facilities could be offered to subscribers, and if the charges were reduced the telephone would be more generally used.

BRICKMAKING NEAR JOHNSTONE.—A new industry is about to be started near Johnstone. Mr. D. R. Adam, Kirkhill Collieries, Cambuslang, is erecting machinery at No. 3 Douglas Pit, Inkermann, for the conversion of the huge bings of blaze into bricks. The works are to be extensive.

HOSPITAL AT CLACKMANNAN.—The Combination Hospital for the county of Clackmannan, situated near New Sauchie, Alloa, has been opened. The buildings are from plans by Messrs. John Melvin & Son. There is an administrative block, also male and female wards, 18 ft. by 14 ft. There are likewise two pavilions, male and female wards, having accommodation for six beds each.

MESSRS. W. SIMONS & Co., of Renfrew, are very busy with the construction of several large dredgers. Those now being built include one for the Mersey Docks Board, one for Natal, one for Trinidad, and two large stationary dredgers for the Government, the order for the latter having been recently booked. Messrs. Lobnitz, of Renfrew, have secured a contract for a steam hopper of large capacity.

PEEBLES.—The Town Council have received a letter from Major Thorburn offering to restore the market cross of Peebles to its old site at the junction of the High Street, Northgate, and Eastgate, enclosing a plan and specification showing how he intended to carry out the work, and asking the Council's permission to proceed. The matter has been remitted to a committee of the whole Council for consideration.

STIRLING STATION.—At a recent meeting of the Stirling Town Council Provost Kinross reported that a deputation had waited on the directors of the Caledonian Railway Company in reference to the condition of Stirling Station, and were informed that they were prepared to widen the platform next the up-line, take away the present centre line of rails, raise both platforms, increase the waiting-room accommodation, and bring in the Forth and Clyde trains on the main line, the new works to cost about £12,000.
