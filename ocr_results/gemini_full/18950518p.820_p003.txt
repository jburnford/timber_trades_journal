FAILURES AND ARRANGEMENTS.
[It must not be overlooked that in many cases
creditors whose names are published in these lists
hold security for their debts, sometimes for the

ABDELA, JACOB, trading as Jacob
Abdela & Co., timber merchant and commission
agent, 61, George Street, and 5, Heywood Street,
Cheetham, Manchester.—The first meeting of the
creditors of the Official Receiver concerned here-
under took place at the offices of the Official
Receiver, Manchester, on Friday last week.
The statement of affairs filed by the bankrupt
showed gross liabilities amounting to
£2,302 9s. 2d. of which £1,490 10s. 7d. was due
to unsecured creditors. The assets under cer-
tain conditions were estimated to produce
£2,526 12s. 4d. The debtor alleged his failure
to have been caused through bad debts. The
Official Receiver reported that the debtor had
been in business for about twenty years.
In 1893 he made an arrangement with
his creditors, under which he paid a
composition of 4s. in the pound on
unsecured liabilities amounting to £6,400. He
became aware of the fact that he was in an insolvent condition until the
beginning of February last, and he stated that
since then he had not contracted any fresh
liabilities. The debtor, in his statement of
affairs, returns as an asset the sum of £2,250,
the estimated value of a contract entered into
with Marco Effiades, of Valona, Turkey. The
bankrupt alleges that he is entitled to timber
valued at £15,000, and when he sells it his share
of the profit will be £1,500, and an extra 5 per
cent. commission on the amount realised. The
Official Receiver said the debtor had assured him
that if he could raise sufficient money to bring
500 tons of this timber to England there would
be an immediate profit of between £400 and £500
to come to the estate. The timber had been cut,
and was now awaiting shipment. There was
also a possibility of some arrangement being
arrived at with the debtor, who knew something about
Turkish affairs with a view to some arrangement
being made. The debtor, in reply to a creditor, said he had the
full consent of the pasha of the district referred
to in Turkey to take away the timber. It was
pointed out that in Turkey the will of the
Sultan was absolute, and if he said the timber
was not to be taken away the debtor would not
be able to remove it. He added that was his
experience. (Laughter.) Eventually a committee
was appointed to meet the debtor and his wife
and talk matters over with a view to an arrange-
ment being arrived at, and the meeting was
adjourned.

HAMMERSLEY & SONS, NOTTINGHAM.
—A private meeting of the creditors of Ham-
mersley & Son, Nottingham, was held at
Nottingham on the 10th inst., Mr. M. Chaplin,
of Derby and Hull, presided. The statement
of affairs presented showed unsecured liabili-
ties £1,481, and the assets were estimated at
£1,628. It was resolved that the debtor's offer
of 7s. 6d. in the pound by instalments at three,
six, 9 and 12 months secured to the satisfaction
of the Chairman and Mr. Byng, be accepted,
subject to the whole of the creditors, who are
in a position to present a bankruptcy petition
against the debtors, accepting this offer within
ten days, the composition to be payable as
follows—the first three instalments at 2s.
each and the fourth at 1s. 6d., the same to be
secured by the joint and several promissory
notes of the debtors and their sureties,
and issued to the creditors within thirty-one
days from the date hereof.

in opening.
