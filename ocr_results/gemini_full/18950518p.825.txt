LEGAL INTELLIGENCE.

BREACH OF CHARTER-PARTY—CHARTERERS' AGENTS AND REPORTING OF VESSEL.
THE “ÆOLUS.”—FRERICH'S v. CRAWFORD & Co.
(Before the Sheriff-Principal at the Glasgow Sheriff Court.)
This action, raised by W. Frerichs, master of the German barque “Æolus,” against Crawford & Co., steamship owners and ship-brokers, 26, Renfield Street, Glasgow, for payment of £20, being balance of freight retained by Messrs. Crawford & Co., who were acting at Glasgow for the charterers of that vessel, which came to Glasgow with a cargo of mahogany consigned to order has been heard. By the terms of the charter-party it was provided that the vessel was to be addressed for inward reporting to the charterers' agents at the port of discharge, and that in default the charterers' agents were to be paid £20 sterling as liquidated damages; but, notwithstanding that provision, the master had given the reporting of the vessel to other shipbrokers, and Messrs. Crawford claimed that they were entitled to be paid the £20, so provided to be paid by the charter-party. There was also a question as to the failure of the vessel to employ the charterers' stevedore as provided by the charter-party. On November 23rd Sheriff Balfour closed the record, on December 21st he allowed a proof before answer, and on January 15th his lordship opened up the record, and allowed the pursuer to amend his pleadings, reserving the question of expenses, of new, closed the record, and allowed a proof of the additional averments.

825

Against this the defenders appealed to the Sheriff-Principal, who has now given judgment. His lordship opens up the record, allows the pursuer to amend his pleadings, and, this having been done, closes the record of new, reserving the question of expenses. His lordship finds that on a true construction of the charter-party the sum of £20 provided therein as liquidated damages for a breach of the stipulation that the ship should be addressed “for inward reporting to charterers' agents at port of discharge, paying usual commission,” was to be treated as ascertained damages payable in the event of a breach of that stipulation, and was not subject to the modification as a penalty, on evidence as to the amount of damage.
His lordship remits to the Sheriff-Substitute for further procedure.

CONTRACTS OPEN.

ARMY CONTRACTS (Edinburgh).—Works—The Secretary of State for War intimates that builders desirous of being applied to when tenders are invited for work in the Scottish Military District should address the General Officer Commanding, 1, Castle Terrace, Edinburgh, giving trade and bank references, and stating whether they wish to tender for (a) jobbing work, painting, and repairs, (b) new works or works up to £5,000 estimated value, or (c) new buildings or works of any magnitude. Applications unaccompanied by references will not be noticed. Firms whose names are already on the War Office list need not apply.

Drawings, specification, and a copy of the conditions and form of contract may be seen on application to the Postmaster at Dundee between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. Bills of quantities have been prepared for the use of the builders by Mr. P. MONTROSE.—House.—For the mason, carpenter, slater, plasterer, plumber, painter, glazier, and iron work of a new house for private patients at Sunnyside, near Montrose, for the Montrose Royal Lunatic Asylum. Plans and general conditions of contract may be seen and schedules of quantities obtained at the office of Mr. Alexander Lyell, solicitor, Montrose, clerk of the Corporation, on and after 17th inst., on payment of £1 1s., which will be repaid on receipt of a bona-fide tender. Mr. Kelly, architect, Aberdeen, will meet intending contractors on the site to point out same, and old material, &c., on 18th inst., at 12 noon. Sealed tenders, marked on the outside “Tender for House for Private Patients,” must be lodged with the Clerk of the Corporation by 26th inst., at 3 p.m.

DUNDEE INSTITUTE OF ARCHITECTURE, SCIENCE, AND ART.—The third ordinary meeting of the Institute for the session was held in the South Kensington Room, Albert Institute, on Monday last, for the exhibition of the drawings submitted for the local competitions of the Institute. The President, Mr. R. Keith, was in the chair, and Mr. Robert Little, architect, Kirkcaldy, was elected a member.
