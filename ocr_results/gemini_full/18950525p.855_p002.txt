ADAMS, WILLIAM POYNER, builder, High Street, Bloxwich, and Ward Street, Walsall, trading as William Adams.—This bankrupt appeared for his public examination at the Walsall Bankruptcy Court on Friday last, before the Registrar. The statement of affairs filed by the debtor disclosed gross liabilities amounting to £649 19s. 8d., of which £579 0s. 9d. was expected to rank against the estate for dividend. The assets, after deducting £70 19s. for the claims of preferential creditors payable in full, were returned at £27 10s. 8d., thus leaving a deficiency of £551 10s. The debtor in the course of his examination by the Official Receiver stated that he was in a situation until 1868, when he commenced business in Ward Street, Walsall, and in May, 1884, he bought a business and plant in High Street, Bloxwich, for £50, where he afterwards went to reside. He alleged his failure to have been caused through bad trade, bad debts, and pressure by creditors. The examination was ordered to be closed.

BROOKER, G. W., builder, Hurst Green, Sussex.—The public examination of this bankrupt took place at the Hastings Bankruptcy Court, on Monday, before the Registrar. The debtor said he commenced business in 1860, when he entered into partnership with his brother-in-law, a man named Baldock. He had no money, but he had a little stock. The partnership did not answer; he supposed it was because they took contracts at too low a price, and was dissolved about 1870, when witness turned over everything, including the liabilities, to his brother-in-law. After that he went into business at Etchingham, but he had to borrow money for that purpose, and he had not since repaid it, as the lender was dead. In 1880 Lady Baines lent him money, and just before she died she made him a present of it. He afterwards borrowed £10 from a broker, who subsequently forgave him the debt. The Official Receiver remarked that the bankrupt seemed to have a splendid set of creditors, they all seemed to forgive him their debts. He first became aware of his insolvency about two years ago, but he continued in business in the hope that times would improve. The examination was finally ordered to be closed.

BURTON, C. E., builder, Bushbury Road, Heath Town.—A meeting of the creditors of this bankrupt was held at the office of the Official Receiver (Mr. E. Pritchard), at Wolverhampton, on Tuesday. The public examination of the bankrupt took place later in the day at the County Court, before Mr. Registrar Sanders. In answer to the Official Receiver, the debtor stated that he had lost money on nearly all his contracts. He admitted that shortly before his bankruptcy he conveyed two houses to his wife, but said he had no idea at the time that he was going to fail. The bankrupt also admitted that he had forgotten to inform the Official Receiver that he had bought six cottages about five or six years ago for £340, and had sold them to a son-in-law for £310. Out of that amount he paid £268 to a building society. The examination was adjourned.

HARDING, WILLIAM, builder, Market Place, Mitcham, and Streatham Road, Streatham.—The statutory meeting of creditors interested under this failure was held at the offices of the London Bankruptcy Court, Railway Approach, London Bridge, on Wednesday, under the presidency of Mr. E. L. Acheson, Assistant Official Receiver. The statement of affairs filed by the debtor showed gross liabilities amounting to £2,222 5s., against which was expected to rank £2,161, with no estate for dividend. The Official Receiver said: The failure to have been caused by the debtor's inability to sell his houses, which were heavily mortgaged. It appeared that the receiving order was made on the debtor's own petition. He had formerly been adjudicated bankrupt in 1888. He was a carpenter by trade, commencing business in June, 1888, as a speculative builder at Bond, Streatham, with a capital of £100 given him by his father. In 1890 he took his present [?] and all his property was heavily mortgaged.
He became aware of his insolvency in 1890, and since then he has contracted further debts to the extent of £16, in connection with some work he did in 1892-3, which was to be paid out of the profits on the work. He had kept a creditor's ledger, cash book, day book, and bill book, but he had not made out any balance-sheets. As there was no offer of composition to submit, it was resolved to leave the matter in the hands of the Official Receiver.

SCAMMELL & SON, timber merchants, Exeter and Teignmouth.—The public examination has been held at Exeter. The Official Receiver said there was no physical inability in Mr. E. T. Scammell to prevent him from attending the Court, he (the Official Receiver) thought he should have been there. The statement which he sent in a declared statement of affairs was a very long account, and he (the Official Receiver) did not think it was necessary. He said it was his health, and secondly his reason for going away was to see his principal creditor, Mr. Stephens. After seeing him, he came to the conclusion that it would be more convenient to reside further up the country, and he went with his uncle led to his taking part in some gold enterprise. This was making from £400 to £500. This foreshadowed some kind of dividend to the creditors, which he (the Official Receiver) hoped would be realised. Mr. Scammell thought it his primary duty to be making money fast, in the interests of his creditors, to stay in Australia.—The examination was adjourned.
