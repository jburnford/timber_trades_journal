JONES, PHILIP, trading as Jones and Sons, builders, 1, Lower Oxford Street, Swansea.—The public examination of the above-named bankrupt came on for hearing at the Swansea Bankruptcy Court on Thursday week, before Mr. Registrar Home. The statement of affairs filed by the bankrupt disclosed gross liabilities amounting to £4,090, of which £531 was expected to rank against the estate for dividend. The assets were estimated at £320. The deficiency was attributed to his present position in business. The debtor stated that he had been through bad speculation. The deficiency had been caused through bad speculation in building, and also to the fact that he was prevented from continuing building operations in consequence of the Corporation refusing to pass his plans. If the Corporation had allowed him to go on with the building, he believed he could have paid all his creditors in full. He should think he lost quite £400 through the action of the Corporation. The examination was ordered to be closed, the Official Receiver remarking that he thought this was a straightforward case of misfortune. The following are creditors :—

SHEEN, DANIEL, builder, Eton Villa, Christleton Road, Chester, trading at White Alley, George Street, Chester.—The Official Receiver for the Chester district has now issued particulars under this failure, from which it appears that the gross liabilities amount to £1,338 8s. 11d., of which £517 16s. 1d. is due to unsecured creditors. The assets are estimated to produce £353 10s. 5d., thus leaving a deficiency of £164. The debtor alleges his failure to have been caused through bad trade, want of capital, and illness in family. A meeting of the creditors interested herein was held at the offices of the Official Receiver, Chester, on Thursday week. The Assistant Official Receiver presided, and stated that the debtor had expressed a wish to bring forward a proposal of composition, but it had not yet been lodged. After some discussion the debtor said if time were allowed him he might be able to bring a proposal for composition forward. He suggested a composition of 7s. 6d. in the pound. The Official Receiver said he thought the debtor would have to make a better offer than that, as the estate on the debtor's own figures showed 12s. in the pound. Eventually it was resolved to adjourn the meeting, in order that the debtor might have an opportunity of bringing forward a composition scheme.

THOMAS, THOMAS, builder, Chandos Road, Redland, formerly carrying on business at 7, Colston Street, Bristol.—An application was made at the Bristol Bankruptcy Court on Friday last for the discharge of the above-named debtor, before Judge Austin. The Official Receiver reported that the receiving order was made upon the debtor's own petition on November 12th, 1891. The proofs admitted amounted to £1,283, and the assets were estimated to produce £353, but they have only realised £180 9s. 3d., and the probable value of the assets not realised was £50. COPE, F. J. C., timber merchant, Derby and Burton-on-Trent.—At the Derby County Court, before his Honour Judge Smyly, on the 23rd of May, an application was made by Mr. McCarta on behalf of Mr. Williams, trustee, for the rejection of a proof for £1,269 by the estate. Mr. McCarta said his client, Mr. Williams, was the owner of certain property, and in 1894 he wished to dispose of some timber by tender. The result was that the tender of a Mr. Laight was accepted, and a contract was entered into between the parties. The purchase-money was set down at £1411, and the point of law as to the effect of a certain clause in the purchase. That clause specified that if by a certain date any trees had not been cut down they would be forfeited to the vendor, notwithstanding that by that time the whole of the purchase money might have been paid. Promissory notes were given and signed by Mr. Cope, but as on the date specified certain timber remained on the property it was urged by Mr. Laight and Mr. Hextall for the appellant that it had become forfeited, in accordance with the terms of the contract. Mr. Lemart, who appeared for the trustee, Mr. Lemart, chartered accountant of Nottingham, having referred to certain clauses in the agreement, said his Honour said he could understand the trustee rejecting a proof against the estate, but at the same time, taking the clause literally, he must come to the conclusion that the trustee was wrong in rejecting the proof for £1,269, with costs for the appellant.
