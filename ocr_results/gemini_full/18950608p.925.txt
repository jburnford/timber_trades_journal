JUNE 8, 1895.
CORRESPONDENCE.
THE GLASGOW WEDNESDAY TIMBER MARKET.
TO THE EDITOR OF THE TIMBER TRADES JOURNAL.
SIR,—It has been long held that it is rather beneath the dignity of the Scotch timber market for its members to hold its weekly market at the corner of Hope Street, as a refuge in inclement weather from the precincts of the Caledonian Rail. Station.
No doubt many of us are old stagers, but some day it might happen that a considerable proportion of the members present among us may be ignominiously turned out.
Cannot some of our public-spirited leaders in the trade do something to remedy this matter?
It is a pity that this large and important trade cannot obtain a permanent meeting-place.
No one would be glad to contribute to an annual subscription for that purpose, as has been suggested to me more than once, if we are still to meet in the open air.
An application to the City Council might obtain us permission to use the front steps of the Municipal Buildings in George Square.
I would most respectfully suggest that possibly the Exchange, if approached, might, for a small annual payment, allow us to meet under the portico in front of that building with its massive pillars.
This would afford us some shelter from the weather, and there is no doubt about it.
Perhaps some of your many readers might suggest a better place than either of these, but at any rate something should be done, and that quickly.
A TIMBER TYRO.

GLASGOW CITY COUNCIL DEAN OF GUILD COURT.
AT a meeting of the Bathgate District Committee of Linlithgow County Council on Tuesday, Mr. Wood brought up the decision of a Dean of Guild Court in connection with the County Council for regulating the building of properties.
Captain Hope, M.P., said that there was no power made for district committees in the Bill.
They had only powers to criticise matters after they were up, but he understood there was a proposal in the new Bill for an amendment to grant similar powers to county authorities in counties as they had in burghs, and these would be an advantage, as they had always experienced difficulty in regulating the building of new properties.

ACTION IN DISPUTE ABOUT BARREL STAVES.
IN the Court of Session this week, judgment was given by Lord Low in an action brought by John H. Douglas & Co., timber merchants, 39, Mildmay Chambers, Bishopsgate Street, London, against James Marr, fish curer, Aberdeen, for payment of £200 12s. 6d., the price of a large quantity of staves supplied to the defender.
In defence it was averred that the wood was purchased by the pursuers to the defender for the special purpose of making herring pickle barrels, and that it turned out to be totally unfit for that purpose or any other purpose for which it could be used, and the defender pleaded that the wood, being disconform to contract, and was of no value, was rejected.
Lord Low was of opinion that the conditions under which the staves were to be supplied were that they should be good and merchantable for the purpose of making pickle barrels.
The question was accordingly one of fact, and on that his lordship was of opinion that the pursuers' staves was absolutely useless for the purpose of making barrels.
His lordship considered that the staves were properly rejected, quay, the defenders had intimated that it was useless for the purpose for which it was intended.
The defender was accordingly assoilzied, with expenses.

PORT INTELLIGENCE.
FRASERBURGH.
Great progress has been made with the harbour improvement works at Fraserburgh, the contractors, Messrs. Price & Wills, of Westminster, having shown a great deal of energy.
The contract includes the deepening of the Balaclava Harbour six feet below low water level, the construction of two new jetties within the harbour, and the extension of the South Breakwater.
Operations were begun on the construction of the concrete wall at the entrance to exclude the water.
This was accomplished with much difficulty as it was in rock and part in clay.
When the wall was almost completed it was subjected to a very violent storm from the south-east, the spray from the seas going fifty feet over the top of the lighthouse.
The structure itself did not suffer any damage from this, although the contractors' plant did to a small extent.

IRVINE.
It is proposed to carry out certain improvements at Irvine Harbour, including an extension of the wharfage.
Mr. Deas, of Glasgow, recommends the erection of a light-tower at the South Perch, and of a coloured light at the lower end of wharf at a probable cost of £700.
He has also reported as to the character of the work which would be suitable in connection with the extension of the wharfage at the upper or eastern end of the harbour.
Messrs. Leslie & Reid, C.E., who had been asked to report as to the probable cost of substituting concrete for timber at part of the wharf near the centre, estimated the cost at the rate of £43 per lineal yard, or £2,580 for the distance specified.

MONTROSE.
The dock still continues closed, greatly to the inconvenience of traders.
At a meeting of the Harbour Trustees a report was read from Mr. Henderson, C.E., Burntisland, on the present position of the work.
Mr. Henderson reported that Messrs. Head, Wrightson & Co., the contractors for the new gates, had completed the contract as far as the work could be done, but that there still remained certain work to be done under their contract when the repair of the dock gates was further advanced.
He stated that he had measured the work done, and submitted it to the contractors, who had expressed themselves satisfied that the measurement was correct.
The total value of the work done amounted to £1,524 4s. 6d.; the value of that which still remained to be done at schedule rates amounted to £30 12s.—a total expenditure when the contract was completed of £1,554 16s. 6d., as compared with the contract amount of £1,601.
In regard to the Montrose Shipbuilding Company's contract for the gates.
Mr. Henderson reported that no work had been done under that contract since he last reported to the Board.
He stated that he had written twice to the manager of the company asking the work to be proceeded with at once, but had received in reply was a post-card to the effect that his letters would be, or had been, laid before the directors of the Shipbuilding Company.
Provost Mitchell asked the meeting if they proposed to take any action in the matter, but, after a brief conversation, it was agreed, in the absence of Mr. Jamieson Paton, Convenor of the Sub-Committee, to defer further consideration on the subject for a month.

GREENOCK HARBOUR BILL. — A report was submitted at the meeting of Greenock Harbour Trust on the new Parliamentary Bill.
When passed the warrant system of warehousing will be introduced.
The Bill is expected to pass this week.
Mr. Denholm said they would be able to issue warrants for goods of all classes to be warehoused in their sheds, timber ponds, or any other part of their works.
They would now be placed in a position for attracting traffic which they had never occupied before; and it must be satisfactory to the trustees, after having been urged to put the clause in the Bill, that they were able to carry it without any opposition at all.
