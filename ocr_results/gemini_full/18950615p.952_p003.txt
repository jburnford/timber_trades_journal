REVIEW.
Great Britain's Colonial and Indian Possessions. London: John Walker & Co., Limited.
This is what it professes to be, viz, "a handy reference guide to the British Empire." It is not complete-what book could be for a shilling? -but it gives in a concise, pocketable form an outline of each colony's history and some idea of their physical geography, together with the latest statistics that are available, and manages in a space of 120 pages to give a great deal of information that should be known to every commercial man. It may be said the knowledge imparted is scrappy but this is a superficial age, and Messrs. Walker's new book will enable many a man to gloss over his ignorance of the further ends of the British Empire.

LEWIS, JOHN, English and Foreign timber merchant, 12, Willes Road and Great Western Saw-mills, Leamington. - At a largelyattended meeting of creditors held at Messrs. Wright & Hassall's office, Leamington, on the 23rd ult., it was stated that the debtor had successfully carried on business for forty years, and his present position was occasioned by losses to the extent of £4,000 owing to the failures of Wm. Kirton & Co., London; A. Hutchinson, Worcester; Jesse J. Bailey, Newport, Mon.; and Robert Burden, Chester. A statement of affairs prepared by Mr. J. F. Burgis, chartered accountant, was submitted, showing liabilities £3,825, assets £7,492-a surplus of £3,667. It was arranged that an assignment should be made, and three trustees were appointed to secure payment to creditors in full by four equal instalments. This was unanimously agreed to, and much sympathy expressed at the debtor's unfortunate position, one creditor postponing her claim.

TORKINGTON, ALFRED, 8, Elwy Street, Rhyl, builder and contractor.-The adjourned public examination of this bankrupt took place at the Bangor Bankruptcy Court on Thursday last, before the Registrar. The statement of affairs filed by the debtor disclosed liabilities amounting to £563 7s. 10d. Replying to questions put by the Assistant Official Receiver the debtor stated that he deposited with his solicitor the lease of a certain property as security when his solicitor knew that he was unable to meet his debts as they became due, and at that time he consulted him about making an arrangement with his creditors. He alleged that his present position to have been caused through losses on contracts and illness. Finally the examination was closed.

TRADE NOTES.
Trade generally continues quiet. The demand for immediate consumpt appears to have been temporarily slightly oversupplied, and to induce forward buying holders are asked to make concessions which are surely reasonable in view of the condition of the market and the large consumption of timber, which contracts already held in the building and ship-building industries have rendered certain. The present state of the market may be regarded as only a temporary lull, and business seem likely to remain firm until the active demand is again experienced.
Arrivals for the week, as will be seen from our other columns, are by no means excessive, although for the reasons above stated a super proportion than usual has probably gone into yard.
We have had no public sales this week. Messrs. Singleton, Dunn, & Co. decided to postpone their sale announced for Wednesday last, but too late for us to notify our readers.
Advices from Canada afford no basis for anything else than firmness in quotations for new goods. Stocks there have not been heavy and are already well cleared. The only elements in favour of lowered quotations are the low rate of freights and rapidity in delivery.
