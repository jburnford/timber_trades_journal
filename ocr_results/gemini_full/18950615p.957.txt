BURGHHEAD.
Burghead Harbour Company, Limited, has been registered at Edinburgh, with a capital of £10,000, in £10 shares (2,500 preference and 7,500 ordinary), to take over as a going concern the port and harbour of Burghead, and to carry on the business of carriers, shipowners, warehouse keepers, &c.
The first directors are Messrs. H. W. Young, Burghead, sole proprietor; W. C. Young, junr., Burghead; R. MacKessack, junr., Elgin, solicitor; J. M. S. Mackay, Elgin, bank agent; James P. Hay, Keith, merchant; T. D. Gordon Duff, of Park, Parkhouse; and J. D. Maclennan, Forres.
The number of directors is not to be less than two nor more than seven.
The first are H. W. Young, W. C. Young, junr., R. MacKessack, H. M. S. Mackay, and J. P. Hay, T. Mackenzie, and J. D. Maclennan.
Qualification, £500.
Registered office, 13, South Charlotte-street, Edinburgh.

GRANGEMOUTH.
Last week there was a very large business going on at the docks owing to the numerous arrivals of steamers with cargoes of timber.
The dock porters were fully employed, and the imports per sailing vessels of deals, battens and deals and battens from the Baltic were also large.
Besides of straw from France.
The arrivals of Continental steam traders landed large and general cargoes, while the outward shipments were also well filled.
The demand for shipping for the week was fair, several boats having been placed for Copenhagen, Cronstadt, Newfairwater, and Cronstadt.
There is a firmer tone in the coal market, and steam tonnage is somewhat scarcer.
The steamer "Kinghorn" has arrived from Antwerp with coal-tar pitch.

IRVINE.
At a meeting of the Irvine Harbour Trust, held last week, under the presidency of Provost Murchie, it was stated that the import trade for the past quarter had been as under:—Pig iron, 1,065 tons; salt, 4,288 tons; iron ore, 10,611 tons; limestone, 10,611 tons; sand, 6,012 tons; and timber, 502 loads, making 33,075 tons.
The exports were:—Coals, 50,223 tons; fire-clay goods, 758 tons; pig iron, 1,000 tons; salt-cake, 86 tons; and general goods, 1,706 tons—total, 59,652 tons.
The income for the quarter amounted to £1,053 1s. 4d., and the expenditure to £1,052 17s. 4d.—leaving a balance of 4s. in favour of the harbour.
The trustees agreed to refer the plans for the proposed improvement of the leading lights to the Northern Lights Commissioners, and to ascertain whether the Commissioners would undertake their erection.

AT GREENOCK.—At a meeting of the Greenock Harbour Committee last Saturday it was agreed to accept the new firm of Russell & Co. to take over the Messrs. Russell & Co.'s shipbuilding yard of Messrs. Russell & Co., as tenants during the continuation of the present lease, which has yet six years to run.
It is stated that, in addition to shipbuilding, the business will include engineering and boilermaking industries.
It is understood that the new firm have secured a large order in the shape of a large sailing ship, which will be proceeded with as soon as it will be possible to get the yard into proper working order.

BUILDING NEWS.
A new police station is to be built at Govanhill, at an estimated cost of £18,000.
The joiners in the Comrie district in the employment of Messrs. P. Comrie & Sons have struck work for an advance of ½d. per hour.
The wages at present are 5d. and 6½d.
The estate of Mr. Alexander Melville, timber merchant, Glasgow, who resided at 2, Belhaven Terrace, has been returned at £3,802 2s. 6d.
At a meeting of the Rutherglen Town Council the recommendation of the Burgh Lands Committee to alter the Townhall Buildings at an expense of £800 was negatived.
The Buildings Committee of the Kilmarnock School Board has been authorised to secure sketch plan and estimate cost of new secondary and technical school proposed to be erected on ground recently feued t Elmbank.
Provost Aitken, Kirkintilloch, laid the memorial stone of the Young Men's Christian Association buildings in Kirkintilloch.
The erection is to occupy a prominent position on the main street, and is to be a two-storey building, with large hall, library, cloak, and other rooms.
The estimated cost is about £1,000.
Last week the Plans Committee of Aberdeen Town Council sanctioned plans of a number of new buildings, including a hall for John Street Evangelical Union congregation, and premises in Mearns Street for the Newcastle Shipping Company.
The value of the new buildings is estimated at between £13,000 and £14,000.
At Ayr Messrs. Bennett & Sons have purchased an old block of buildings fronting Wellington Square and Fullarton Street.
The purchasers intend to erect on it business buildings, four stories high, at a cost of £15,000.
Miss Jane Houldsworth, Roselle, has also had plans prepared of a block of buildings in Newton, specially for working people, which are to cost several thousands.
At a largely-attended meeting of joiners held in the Tron Hall, last week, the following resolution was unanimously adopted :—"That this meeting regrets to learn that the present method of arranging matters between employers and employed, which hitherto has proved so satisfactory to all concerned, may now be considered at an end, through the master builders refusing to submit the eight hours question, now in dispute, to the arbiter appointed to act in these matters under the Board of Conciliation rules, notwithstanding the fact that they offered to concede, in the early spring, a forty-eight hours week, and, further, that this meeting reaffirms its adherence to th eight hours demand, and instructs the United Trade Committee, in the altered circumstances, to take immediate steps to ascertain otherwise whether the men are prepared to enforce their claims in the only way left open to them."
