SHIPBUILDING.
Messrs. Robert Duncan & Co., Port Glasgow, have contracted to build a paddle-steamer for cattle and passenger traffic at the Straits of Gibraltar. The machinery will be supplied by Messrs. Rankin & Blackmore, Greenock.
The Fairfield Shipbuilding and Engineering Company, Limited, have contracted to build and engine a screw steamer for the Scottish Oriental Steam Navigation Company, Limited.
It is rumoured in Glasgow that the Union Steamship Company of New Zealand are in the market for five steamers of about 250 ft. in length for their coasting trade.
Clyde shipbuilders are hopeful of securing a good share of twenty additional torpedo destroyers for Her Majesty’s Government. They are to have a mean speed of thirty knots on three hours’ trial.

BUILDING NEWS.
The Post Office authorities have in contemplation the erection of entirely new buildings at Kirkcaldy, to meet the requirements of the department in the town.
* * *
Messrs. J. & C. Hay, Long Wynd, Dundee, have received intimation that their offer for the new Dundee Post Office building has been accepted. The contract price is £21,366, and the stone to be used in the building will be secured from Burntisland.
* * *
On Saturday Provost Aitken, Kirkintilloch, laid the memorial stone of the Young Men’s Christian Association buildings in the town. The premises are in the main street, and will be a two-story building, with large hall, library, cloak, and other rooms. The estimated cost is £1,000.
* * *
At the Coatbridge Dean of Guild Court, Dean of Guild Wilson presiding, plans have been passed for the erection of new property, the estimated value of which was upwards of £10,000. Amongst the linings was one for the erection of additions to the Technical School metallurgical department, and for the enclosing of a court at Gartsherrie Institute as a suite of halls.
* * *
At the holding of the Ayr Dean of Guild Court warrants were granted to David Campbell for a villa in Blackburn Road, and to Robert Harvey for a villa in St. Leonards. The burgh surveyor reported that during the past year seventy-nine linings had been granted, as compared with seventy-eight in the previous year. The fees paid were £63 7s. 6d. for the preceding year, as against £63 8s. The value of the buildings passed by the Court was £72,000, an increase of £4,000 over the previous year.
* * *
At the holding of the Partick Dean of Guild Court, Messrs. Alex. M’Ewan & Sons, builders, Partick, were granted lining to erect seven tenements of dwelling-houses in Church Street and Thomson Street, the estimated cost being £8,000; Peter M’Kissock & Sons, to erect a villa in Park Gardens North at a cost of £1,200, and twelve self-contained houses in Crow Road at a cost of £3,000; George Anderson builder, Whiteinch, to erect four cottages in Park Street, Whiteinch, at an estimated cost of £1,200.
* * *
The School Board of Eastwood Parish met to receive the estimates for the work of rebuilding Thornliebank School, which was burned down some months ago. Sir John Stirling-Maxwell, Bart., of Pollok, presided, and Mr. Rowan, architect, and Mr. Watson, measurer, were in attendance. The lowest estimates totalled £5,634, compared with £5,500 anticipated by the Board. After some discussion, it was agreed that the measurer should check the three lowest estimates for each class of work, and report to a special meeting. The Board also discussed the advisability of rebuilding the tower, which, according to the architect, would cost from £200 to £250. The question was left over till Monday.
