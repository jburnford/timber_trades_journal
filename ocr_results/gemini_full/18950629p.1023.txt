INTELLIGENCE.
DUNDEE.
The Works Committee of Dundee Harbour Board have had the following matters under consideration:—A plan and specification for the extension of the west round head, Victoria Dock, from "Amsterdam" (s), were considered and approved of. The offer of Messrs. Charles Fergusson, Tayport, for a supply of whinstone for the graving docks at the price of 2s. 1d. per ton was accepted as the most favourable offer. Offers were produced for the erection of the towers and lighthouses at Buddonness, the offer of Mr. James Scott, Carnoustie, amounting to £1,280, being accepted. The Engineer submitted a report on the alterations on the ground at the east end of Camperdown Dock, and also on the ground occupied by Messrs. James Fleming & Co. It was agreed that a row of piles be driven in at the west side of the enclosed space at Stannergate for the better preservation of rafts of timber.

GREENOCK.
The Greenock Harbour Bill, which has successfully passed through the House of Lords, has now been considered and passed by the Committee of the House of Commons on unopposed Bills. The preamble was proved by Provost Shankland and Mr. George Macfarlane, clerk to the Harbour Trust.

BUILDING NEWS.
Employment is good in all branches of the building trade throughout the west of Scotland.
* * *
Returns from nine branches of unions in the wood-working and furnishing trades show employment to be fairly good, with 1.6 per cent. idle.
* * *
In the Edinburgh district the painters, masons, bricklayers, and glaziers report employment as good, the plumbers, joiners, and lath-splitters as fair.
* * *
The cabinet-makers report employment as good; the saw-millers as moderate, with 2 per cent. idle; the coachmakers as fair with 1 per cent. idle; the wood-carvers as slightly improved; the wood-turners as steady.
* * *
GLASGOW.—It was agreed some time ago to build a new stone bridge across the Clyde at Govan Street with the material of Glasgow Bridge, which is at present being taken down. That scheme, however, had to be abandoned, owing to a decision to rebuild Glasgow Bridge entirely with granite, and to use the best of the old material in its reconstruction. At a meeting of the Bridges Committee of the Town Council it was remitted to Mr. White, the master of works, to prepare plans and get estimates for the erection of a new stone bridge to be built at Govan Street, at the Green. It was also decided to prepare plans and get estimates for the erection of a suspension bridge further up at the bend of the river leading across to Oatlands Square.
* * *
After undergoing extensive repairs, which will make it one of the finest country churches in the west of Scotland, Roseneath church will be opened next Sabbath.
* * *
A special meeting of Paisley Dean of Guild Court was held on Wednesday, when was granted the Association for Improving the Condition of the Poor to make additions to their fire-lighter factory at M'Dowall Street.
* * *
On Saturday forenoon the foundation stone of the new school at Alloway was laid by Miss Dunlop, of Doonside. The school, which is situated on the Doonholm Road, and near to Burns's Cottage, will accommodate 256 scholars. The estimated cost is £2,000.
* * *
At Ayr Dean of Guild Court last week plans were passed for erection by Miss Hay-Boyd, of Townend, Penpont, of a block of workmen's houses in Allison Street, at the estimated cost of £2,000, and for the erection of a cottage at 51, Allison Street, by William Gouldie.
* * *
At Pollokshaws Dean of Guild Court at its fortnightly sitting on Wednesday, the following applications were granted to William Ralston, upholsterer, Ulster Buildings, Pollokshaws, to erect a three-story tenement of one, two, and three rooms and kitchen, at 166, King Street; and Michael M'Kenzie, Shawlands, to re-erect Govan Paper Mill, recently destroyed by fire.
* * *
The Alyth Public School additions include five new rooms, two cloakrooms, and extensive corridors, planned so that the head master can go round the whole school and have a view through the glass doors of the work going on in every department. The additional accommodation is for about 250 scholars, and the whole school when completed will admit at an approximate calculation, of 550 scholars. The architect of the additions is Mr. John Carver, Meigle, who also designed the original building. The estimated cost of the addition is somewhere about £2,100.
