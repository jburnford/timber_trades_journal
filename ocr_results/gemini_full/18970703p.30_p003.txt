BEALE, WILLIAM, builder, Battersea, S.W.—The bankrupt attended at the Wandsworth Bankruptcy Court last week before Mr. Registrar Willoughby for his adjourned public examination. Mr. G. H. Acheson, Assistant Official Receiver, represented the Board of Trade; Mr. W. Inderwick, barrister-at-law, appeared for the trustees (R. J. Ward & Co.); and Mr. Bankrupt said the information he gave on April 2nd last, in his preliminary examination, was correct, and the statement of affairs disclosed full particulars of liabilities and assets. He had been engaged in various businesses for the last 27 years in connection with the Birkbeck Bank and similar societies. He kept no book of account and received detailed accounts from the societies, which were regularly audited, and he had handed them over to the Official Receiver. Up to 1894 he received no accounts to keep, only an account was submitted to him weekly, and he used to sign it as correct. The bank's accountants balanced his books in 1895 and 1896 and prepared balance-sheets. He had been frequently visited by the sheriff, and filed his petition on March 31st, when the sheriff was in possession. He was examined in 1896 he was insolvent to the extent of £1,000, 8s. That was ascertained by the accountants. He personally prepared no accounts, as the bank did it, and they had the full account of his general trade and of his transactions with the bank only. He had also been trading at a profit, although at the same time have made a very large loss, which was all swallowed up by interest on mortgages. According to the last year's accounts he had established £2,300 for interest and other liabilities. The bank account was a loss of £5,000, but he considered that he was in the most reliable, as he was quite sure the way the accountants prepared it. One of the statements, about which he sought leave and had it examined, was as far as he could say it. For the past three years the bank had made a deficit at £6,000, and in 1894 the whole of the properties were mortgaged, and they found their value was under-estimated by their own idea of value from the estimate of a surveyor. Stock had been taken regularly during the last three years, and he had let it go through, it for his own satisfaction and for the information of the bank. He admitted that the business with the bank was under his control, and he had been in their employ for 27 years, which was a separate account. Could not say that he was no more slave than servant to him he had worked for them all the time. Mr. Inderwick: I should ask that the bankrupt be required to file a cash account and communicate with the trustees, and know the position of affairs. The Bank were the largest creditors, and it was proved that the whole of the property was inpossess[ion] of the bank, and that the debtor had no interest in it. The investigation had been a long one, and until August no dividend could be paid.
