JULY 3, 1897.
A NEW BARONET.
The list of Jubilee honours includes the name of Mr. John Gilmour, of Edinburgh. Mr. John Gilmour, son of John Gilmour of Montrave, is connected with a well-known Glasgow family, and is about fifty years of age. His father was a partner in the firm of Pollock, Gilmour & Co., Union Street, Glasgow, a well-known firm of timber brokers, which is now non-existent. He succeeded to the estates of Montrave and Lundin about fifteen years ago, and shortly afterwards rebuilt the mansion house, which takes rank among the most magnificent residences in the county, if not in Scotland. Before his father's death the new baronet had closely identified himself with the agricultural pursuits, and particularly with the breeding of horses for pleasure and agricultural purposes. He founded the famous stud of Montrave Clydesdales, making his first purchase at the Auchendannan sale in August, 1879; but he did not go seriously into the business until some years later, when he bought a large number of the best brood mares of the breed, among which he paid a high price for Moss Rose, for which he was celebrated. At the close of the Highland and Agricultural Society's show held in Glasgow in 1888 he acquired the champion two-year-old colt Prince of Albion, for which he paid £3,000. As indicated, however, his attention was not solely confined to Clydesdale horses. A number of the farms on his estates came into his hands on account of agricultural depression, and in consequence he became an extensive farmer. This led him to undertake the breeding of almost all classes of farm stock on an extensive scale. He possesses one of the best herds of shorthorn cattle in Scotland, and for some time has occupied a leading position at the Smithfield fat stock shows with both cattle and sheep. For upwards of ten years Mr. Gilmour has occupied an important position on the board of the Highland and Agricultural Society. He has been twice president of the Clydesdale Horse Society.
