JULY 10, 1897. 71
CLYDE TRUST.
At the meeting of the Clyde Trustees on Monday the Lord Provost stated that the revenue of the Trust for the year ending June 30th amounted to £410,084, being the largest annual amount in the history of the Trust.
The minutes of the Harbour Committee contained a report by the Sub-Committee on Timber Trade Accommodation, the closing paragraph of which was as follows: —"The sub-committee, having fully considered the subject of affording additional storage, recommend that the vacant ground on the south side of Govan Road be utilised for this purpose, and that on the condition that the wood be removed on a year's notice, and on back part on six months' notice, the total area of the ground proposed to be thus set apart being 17,377 square yards; and that the Trustees should, during their pleasure, give the free use of one of their powerful tug engines, with bogies, to carry, as discharged, deals by rails to this ground." Mr. George Browne moved the approval of the minutes. Mr. T. C. Guthrie proposed as an amendment that there should be added to the closing paragraph in the report on timber accommodation the words, "provided a ship is discharged at the south quay of Cessnock Dock." Mr. Shearer seconded the amendment. Mr. Thomas Mason moved as another amendment, "that the words 'free use' be ommitted from the closing paragraph in the report." Mr. Walter Paton seconded this amendment. On a vote being taken as between the two amendments, that of Mr. Guthrie was carried by 11 to 8. The motion was then put against Mr. Guthrie's amendment, when the latter was carried by 11 to 8.
