NON-FLAMMABLE WOOD.—On Saturday, at Hurlingham, an exhibition designed to show the security against fire of buildings constructed of timber treated by the British Non-Flammable Wood Company's process was conducted on their grounds.
It was a severe trial which took place at the Royal Naval Works, before the officials of Her Majesty's Office of Works, and which was witnessed by the Prince of Wales, and reported in the TIMBER TRADES JOURNAL at the time.
For the present experiment two buildings, each 12 ft. square and 30 ft. in height, had been erected, similar in the ornamental weather-boarding of which one was made of "treated" wood, while the other of ordinary timber.
Each was furnished with a chimney, for the purpose of creating a strong draught and facilitating combustion.
Fires were lit in the houses, on the windward side, with firewood and shavings, which were ignited simultaneously.
In five minutes the lining of ordinary timber was blazing, and in about half an hour it had been reduced to ashes.
But the other structure was perfect, save for the charring of the wood on the side exposed to the direct action of the flames.
A further test had been applied by igniting a pile of shavings and pots inside, but again the effect was confined to the wood, which did not catch fire.
Indeed, a box made of the treated wood which had been placed in the heart of the firewood, was afterwards found to be practically uninjured, and the papers which it contained were handed to the spectators.
The experiments were witnessed by the Duke of Teck, Lord Charles Beresford, and a large and fashionable company.

DEAL PORTERS AT FLEETWOOD.
WITH reference to the Cardiff one, has been the action of the deal porters at Fleetwood.
The merchants' porters recently demanded an advance of a penny per standard, the stevedores' men struck in sympathy, with the result that the port was blocked.
The Federation was appealed to, but in the meantime the men have been induced to work on most of the boats.
