FAILURES AND ARRANGEMENTS.
[It must not be overlooked that in many cases creditors whose names are published in these lists hold security for their debts, sometimes for the full amount.]
BEACOCK, JOHN, TON, builder, Scunthorpe. — The public examination of this debtor took place last week, at the Grimsby Bankruptcy Court, before the Registrar. The statement of affairs showed a deficiency of £188. Replying to questions, the debtor stated that he commenced business about five years ago, without any capital of his own, and his profits had amounted to about £1 per week, whereas his household expenses had amounted to 23s. or 24s. per week. An order for a proper set of books of account was finally ordered to be kept, and the examination was adjourned until the next Court.

BENTLEY, SAMUEL, joiner, Webster Lane, Scholes, Bradford.—The above-named debtor was publicly examined at the Bradford Bankruptcy Court, last week, before the Registrar. The statement of affairs filed by the bankrupt disclosed liabilities amounting to £481 2s. 5d., and the assets were estimated to produce £109 18s. 5d. The debtor, in answer to questions put by the Official Receiver, stated that he attributed his failure to bad debts, competition, want of capital, and expenses caused through illness and death in his family. He commenced business about 35 years ago, and had a capital of £40 at the time. For the first fifteen years he made a good living at it, but for the last twenty years he had been embarrassed in his financial affairs. He built himself a house and workshop, but they were now heavily mortgaged. The principal creditors, Harrison & Singleton, of Bradford, had been running on for about 25 years. The debtor was eventually allowed to pass his examination.
DAVIES, ALLAN, late builder, carrying on business at 1, Gorse Hill, and Rodbourne.—The first meeting of the creditors interested hereunder was held at the Official Receiver's offices, Swindon, last week. The statement of affairs filed by the bankrupt disclosed gross liabilities amounting to £1,672 14s. 8d., of which £1,113 5s. was fully secured, the value of the securities being estimated at £1,320. The unsecured creditors were put down at £537 9s. 4d., thus leaving a deficiency of £342 14s. Debtor alleged his failure to have been caused through building houses he could not sell. The Official Receiver reported that the receiving order was made upon the petition of a creditor, the act of bankruptcy being that the debtor had given notice that he had suspended, or was about to suspend, payment of his debts. As debtor was unable to submit any offer of composition to the creditors he was adjudicated bankrupt. The Official Receiver further stated that the bankrupt had recently paid money to one of his creditors, and that the transaction would have to be fully investigated. Eventually the estate was left in the hands of the Official Receiver for summary administration. The debtor subsequently appeared for public examination at the Swindon Bankruptcy Court, when, in reply to questions, he stated that in 1893, when he was in partnership with another person, they executed a deed of assignment for the benefit of their creditors, under which they paid a composition of 2s. 6d. in the pound. He afterwards commenced business on his own account without any capital, as a speculative builder. He had not kept a proper set of books of account. His estate would not pay a dividend of 10s. in the pound to his unsecured creditors. He called a meeting of his creditors in April last, at which he offered a composition of 2s. 6d. in the pound, but it was not accepted. It was upon that act of bankruptcy that the petition was filed by a creditor. He had drawn from the business 30s. per week for household expenses, which was less than he could make as a journeyman. He was £200 to the bad twelve months ago. The examination was adjourned until the next court.
