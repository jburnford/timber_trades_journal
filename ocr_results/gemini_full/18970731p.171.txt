1897, at 12 o'clock noon,

LEITH.
The importation of wood goods during the
past week has been on a reduced scale, the
arrivals consisting of 11,904 deals and bat-
tens per "Fannie," from Kotka; 292 deals
per "Penelope" (s), from Danzig; 3,662 pine
deals and boards and 170 logs birch per
"Montreal" (s), from Montreal; 34,033 deals
and battens per "Petersburg" (s), from St.
Petersburg; 234 oak logs and 28,379 oak
planks per "Dwina" (s), from Libau; 11,917
deals and 7,654 pieces lathwood per
"Penelope" (s), from Riga; 6,817
deals and battens and 7,458 pieces lathwood
per "Carl," from Cronstadt.
It is interesting to note that for the first
seven months of this year there has been no
direct importation of wood goods
from Quebec. With the same period of 1896
there is a shortage of 2,472 loads, the figures
being 46,170 loads against 48,642 for the
seven months of 1896. With an active
trade going on this looks healthy,
and with the later re-opening of the naviga-
tion a balance may possibly account for the
deficiency, and there is of course ample time
to turn the scale the other way.
Owing to the trade holidays this week, extend-
ing from Thursday until Tuesday of next
week, business is naturally restricted business for
the present, but this will doubtless be fully
restored when work being resumed.

PRESSURE upon our space this week obliges
us to hold over the reports of the Fraserburgh,
Granton, Greenock, Inverness,
Kirkcaldy, Leith, Stornoway, and Wick imports.

IN PREPARATION for their auction, which
is to be held at Queen's Dock on August
11th, John W. Woyka & Co. have been busy
cataloguing the varied imports to be included
in the sale, and they have issued a preli-
minary list, which comprises African maho-
gany, Honduras mahogany, walnut and
Canary whitewood, birch and ash, oak boards
and squares, Quebec yellow pine deals, &c.
A noteworthy feature of this sale will be
the fact that for the first time in the timber
trade of Scotland African mahogany will
be offered by public auction on account of
the original importers. About 100 logs
have arrived already via Liverpool, there
being no direct line to Glasgow from the
West Coast of Africa; and these logs have
been piled and lotted partly in John W.
Woyka & Co.'s stores, at the foot of
Finnieston Street, and partly on the usual
piling ground at Queen's Dock.
