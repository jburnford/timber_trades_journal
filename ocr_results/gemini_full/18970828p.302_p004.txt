GAZETTE.
FAILURES AND ARRANGEMENTS.
[It must not be overlooked that in many cases creditors whose names are published in the lists hold security for their debts, sometimes for the full amount.]

ASHWORTH, JOHN, builder, May Cottage, Davyhulme, Manchester, formerly carrying on business at King Street and Dalton Street, Manchester.—The statutory meeting of the creditors interested under this failure took place on Tuesday at the offices of the Official Receiver, Manchester. The statement of affairs filed by the Official Receiver showed liabilities amounting to £1,991 17s. 1d., and assets estimated to produce £696 1s. 11d., thus leaving a deficiency of £1,295 15s. 2d. It appeared from the report and observations of the Official Receiver that the bankrupt commenced business twelve years ago with a capital of £100, and he alleged his failure to have been caused through depreciation in the value of house property. The debtor, in reply to questions, admitted that he had not kept proper books of account, and as a rule had only kept memoranda. Eventually it was decided to appoint Mr. Townley Trotter, Chartered Accountant, as Trustee, to act in conjunction with a committee of inspection, which was also appointed.

EVERALL, W. R. & R. E., builders, The Priory, Shrewsbury.—The public examination of the above-named debtor took place at the Shire Hall, Shrewsbury, on Tuesday, before the Registrar. The Official Receiver stated that according to the debtors' statement of affairs the assets were estimated to produce more than the liabilities, but he did not think the book debts would realise the amount they were returned at. The debtors alleged their failure to have been caused through want of sufficient capital, bad debts, and loss of £200 through competition. The receiving order was made on their own petition in consequence of an execution being levied upon them by a trade creditor. The examination was eventually ordered to be closed.

It was elicited that about 12 years ago the debtor compromised with all his creditors but one, and to him he gave a charge upon a reversion under his father's will, expectant on the death of his mother. That creditor was now the largest creditor, his debt amounting to upwards of £700. The furniture was claimed by the debtor's wife. The causes of failure were attributed to illness of the debtor, improvements to the landlord's property, law prices, and underselling. The debtor's turnover amounted to about £3,000 per annum. The debtor had not been pressed by any of his creditors, and the bank having refused to allow him an overdraft, he consulted Mr. King, solicitor, Bristol, who advised him to at once call his creditors together.

The debtor's solicitor offered a composition of 10s. in the pound at two, four, six, and eight months, and would endeavour to find guarantee for the last two instalments, but the creditors were of opinion that it was undesirable to allow the debtor to continue to trade with the creditors' money, and that it would be best to wind up the estate forthwith. It was therefore resolved that the debtor execute a deed of assignment in favour of Mr E. T. Collins, C.A., with Messrs. HOLLAND, JOHN THOS., coach-builder, 32, Victoria Road, Battersea.—This debtor attended at the Wandsworth Bankruptcy Court, before Mr. Registrar Willoughby, for his public examination, but the Assistant Official Receiver applied for an adjournment on the ground that the first meeting was not yet concluded. The creditors refused to accept a proposal for a composition, and the meeting was adjourned to enable the offer to be amended. The estate was a valuable one, showing 20s. in the pound or more, and if the amended offer was not accepted it was a case in which a trustee would be appointed. The debtor said only one creditor refused his previous offer. The Assistant Official Receiver said the fact was Mr. Holland offered 10s. in the pound when his estate showed 20s. The debtor said that was not so; it was simply an attempt to ruin him. His father had come forward to prevent that, and he was now offering 15s. in the pound cash at a month. Adjourned until Oct 7.

PEARCE, GEORGE WM., builder, 104, Sarsfield Road, Balham.—This bankrupt attended at the Wandsworth Bankruptcy Court on Thursday, before Mr. Registrar Willoughby, for adjourned public examination. Mr. Clarkson for the trustee said the Court had ordered a cash account, but the one filed did not contain the necessary details. Some of the property was in the wife's name, and it was understood that she was willing to hand the property up. The same had been sold, but the trustee desired that the purchase should be completed before the examination closed. He therefore asked for an adjournment until October 7th. Granted.

ROSSITER, JOHN WESLEY, cabinet maker, Frome.—The public examination of this debtor took place at the last sitting of the Frome Bankruptcy Court, when the debtor, in reply to questions put by the Official Receiver, stated that he commenced business 26 years ago. He effected an arrangement with his creditors 20 years ago, under which he paid a composition of 7s. 6d. in the pound on liabilities amounting to £500. For a long time past he had been pressed by his creditors in the County Court, and he had been unable to do work, in consequence of his inability to get material from the timber merchants. Eventually the examination was adjourned for a month.

STATHAM, ALFRED ERNEST, & WHITBY, JOSEPH, builders and contractors, 8A, Broad Street, Pendleton, Manchester (trading under the style of Statham & Whitby).—The first meeting of the creditors was held at the offices of the Official Receiver, Manchester, on Friday. The statement of affairs filed by the debtors disclosed liabilities amounting to £3,568, and assets estimated to produce £2,235, thus leaving a deficiency of £1,332. The Official Receiver, after reading over the proofs of debt lodged, said there was nothing in the matter, except that both the debtors had previously made arrangements with their creditors, and that Mrs. Whitby claimed practically the whole of the household furniture at 2, Blenheim Place, on the ground that she had purchased it since 1882 out of money left to her by her relations.

TATE, JOHN, joiner, Norton.—This debtor appeared for his public examination at the last sitting of the Stockton Bankruptcy Court before the Registrar. Replying to questions, the debtor stated that he had filed a statement of affairs showing a deficiency of £225. He had built a house for his wife at Norton. His wife had saved money to pay for the house and to purchase several acres of land out of her own earnings as a schoolmistress. He denied that he had built the house to sell as a speculation. Eventually the examination was ordered to be adjourned until the next Court.

YOUNG, WILLIAM, builder, Bexhill.—This debtor made an application for his discharge at the Hastings Bankruptcy Court on Monday, before his Honour Judge Martineau. His Honour said he noticed in the Official Receiver's report that the estimated value of the assets was £2, and that as a matter of fact they had realised £1 12s. Mr. Atkinson, who appeared for the debtor, admitted that the assets were very small, but the debtor had been an honest man, and wished to make a fresh start. Since his failure he had been working as a journeyman carpenter. His Honour: Don't you think it would be wiser for the debtor to remain a journeyman carpenter? Mr Atkinson: He thinks with his previous experience he can do better. His failure occurred four years ago. Eventually his Honour suspended his discharge for two years.
