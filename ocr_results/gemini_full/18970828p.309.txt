and the cargo goes into consumers' hands. The
"Sardinian" (s), of Bathurst, N.B., 1,609 tons,

spruce deals for Allison, Cousland & Co.

LEITH.
The imports this week are comparatively
light, consisting of 10,839 pieces deals
per "Penelope" (s), from Riga; 30,120
deals and battens per "Petersburg" (s),
from St. Petersburg; 290 logs oak, 7,360
oak staves, and 7,360 deals and battens
per "Dwina" (s), from Libau; 10,996
deals per "Frigga," from Rafso, and
31,826 deals and battens per "Petersburg"
(s), from St. Petersburg. Garland & Roger
held a public sale on Tuesday, 24th inst, at their
Railway Arch Yard, Edinburgh Dock. The catalogue
consisted of both a very large and varied assortment
of American and Baltic goods, and there was a
considerable business done in mahogany,
cedar, walnut, ash, oak, and pitch
pine timber, as well as Baltic deals
and battens. Prices were well maintained
for all the better qualities, but
only oddments and inferior goods were
allowed to go cheap. The great variety of
goods offered brought a large company
together.
Failing to get assistance from employers
in other districts, the master masons in the
Edinburgh and Leith district have been
obliged to concede the men's demand for an
eight-hours day, and the strike is consequently
at an end; but such an important
question cannot be considered to be definitely
settled by this local concession. It is
will have to be fought out later on. It is
stated on pretty good authority that the
engineering firms in the East of Scotland
are certain to join the Employers' Federation
in locking out their men unless a settlement
of the dispute is shortly arranged.

THE SCOTCH BUILDING TRADE AND THE
EIGHT-HOURS DAY.—The men engaged in
the building trade at Edinburgh and Leith
who came out on strike seventeen days ago
for a reduction of hours returned to work on
Monday, the masters having conceded an
eight-hours day. The Edinburgh master
joiners have also agreed to increase the wages
of their men from 8½d. to 9d. per hour, commencing
on Monday, September 6th.
Mr. COOPER, general manager of the
Glasgow and South-Western Railway Company,
and other officials, visited Port
Glasgow on August 2, where they were
met by Provost Rodger, the Town Clerk,
and several members of the town council for
the purpose of examining the ground near
to the line of rails of this company running
past Port Glasgow, with the object of recommending
a suitable site for the construction
of a passenger station. The town
council unanimously voted in favour of a
new station, and in doing so represented
the wishes of the community.

SERIOUS ACCIDENT AT PETERHEAD.—A most unfortunate accident occurred last week in the boatbuilding yard, in Bridge Street, occupied by Mr. W. Birnie. It appears that while William Cowie, an old man, aged 80, residing in Wallace Street, Peterhead, was assisting the sawyer to cut a log he accidentally allowed the lower part of his right arm to come in contact with the saw, with the result that the limb was practically cut off, the hand hanging by only a part of the flesh. Medical attendance was at once secured, and Drs. Anderson and Middleton, who were called, found it necessary to amputate the lower part of the arm immediately above the wrist. The unfortunate man is progressing as favourably as could be expected.
