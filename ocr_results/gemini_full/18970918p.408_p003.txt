CAPPS, ROBERT CARTER, AND GEORGE WARD, lately trading as Capps & Ward, yacht and boat builders, Lowestoft.-The public examination of the above-named bankrupts came on for hearing at the Yarmouth Bankruptcy Court on Tuesday before Mr. Registrar Worlledge. The statement of affairs filed by the debtors disclosed liabilities amounting to £1,020, and assets estimated to produce £1,020. In answer to questions put by the Official Receiver, Capps stated that he was formerly foreman to Mr. S. Allerton, and he entered into partnership with the other debtor in 1892. He had no capital of his own at the time, but some friends to back him up, but the partnership deed provided that his part was to put £400 into the business, and each partner was to draw 36s. per week and half share of the profits, which were to be ascertained yearly. They hired a building yard and spent £300 to £400 in erecting sheds and shops, thus setting fast all their capital, so that they only repaired smacks, but afterwards they took to building them. Ward kept the books, but no cash book had been kept, and no record of their receipts and payments. No yearly statement of profits was ever made out because they did not think there were any, owing to their having sunk all their capital on their yard. In December, 1893, there was a flood which seriously damaged their yard and stock, and in that year they went to the bad, probably to the extent of £500. In 1896 they built the "Mistral" and "Glance" for a Mr. Hellyer, the price being £1,000 odd for the pair, but the boats cost about £1,600 to build. He thought the total of work in 1897, and they took a new partner to carry it out. In 1895 their liabilities amounted to £1,100, and in 1896 they were estimated to £1,300. In 1895 they increased their own drawings to £2 per week each.
