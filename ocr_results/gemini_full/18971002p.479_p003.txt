GAZETTE.
FAILURES AND ARRANGEMENTS.
It must not be overlooked that in many cases creditors whose names are published in these lists hold security for their debts, sometimes for the full amount.

AKEHURST, FRANK, builder, Windsor.—The public examination of the above-named debtor took place before the Registrar, at the sitting of the Windsor Bankruptcy Court. A statement of affairs filed by the debtor showed gross liabilities amounting to £317. The assets were estimated to produce £61 5s. 10d. Replying to questions put by the Official Receiver, the debtor stated that he commenced business in Alexandra Road, Windsor, in February, 1881, and in 1883 removed to Dagmar Road. In January, 1887, his liabilities amounted to £500, and he paid a composition of 7s. 6d in the £ to his creditors. In 1890 he again failed, with liabilities amounting to £100 or £500, and he paid his creditors on that occasion 14s. 6d. in the £. He had a capital of £8 when he commenced in 1881, which was advanced to him by his wife. He had kept day book, ledger, and wages book but no cash book. He kept no account of his creditors, and depended upon his invoices and receipts. He had never prepared a balance-sheet at any particular time. He alleged his present position to have been caused through law costs in connection with several actions brought against him by creditors, and losses on contracts. He had really been insolvent since 1893, and at the time of the receiving order an execution had been levied and the high bailiff was in possession. He had continued in business, hoping trade would improve and enable him to pull through. His household expenses had amounted to about £100 per annum. Eventually the examination was ordered to be adjourned.
