LEITH.
There is a marked diminution in the imports of wood goods this week, but further large arrivals are expected immediately. The imports of the last seven days consist merely of 304 rickers, per "(s), from St. Petersburg; 15,146 deals and battens, and 18,157 pcs. lathwood per "Dartmore" (s), from Riga, and per "Rosa & Polly," from Arendal; 4,612 battens per "Olga," from Laurvig; 4,333 deals and battens, and 3 1/2 fathoms lathwood per "Ydeale," from Homestrand.
With the closing week of the quarter there is as usual a falling-off to some extent in the orders for wood goods, but the prospects continue good, and with the advent of the new quarter this temporary slackness will be fully made up. September, on the whole, has been a busy month, both in receiving and delivering, the masons' strike having left a considerable leeway to be made up in the execution of orders for buildings in course of erection.

FORESTRY IN INDIA.
In early times the greater part of India was covered with forest, but the land, as cleared for cultivation was, for the most part, denuded by over-cutting and over-grazing with burning. If denudation has not affected the climate generally, it has without doubt resulted in the drying up of springs and streams rising within the areas deprived of the shelter of a crop of trees, and this is a serious matter in connection with the question of irrigation by canals fed from rivers which are not snow fed, as well as on localities where damage has resulted from the formation of ravines and torrents. The permanence of the supply of timber and other forest produce for the use of the native population and for State purposes has also been endangered.
When the Government wished to take action its powers were found to be inadequate for the destructive usages of the people had come to be regarded as unassailable rights, and it was necessary to pass a special Forest Law, which, among other things, provided for the formation of reserved forests, after a full inquiry had been made into claims, and for the regulation of rights within limits which would not endanger the permanent maintenance of the forest. The work of settlement is now approaching completion in several provinces.
MINISTER OF AGRICULTURE AND THE HAILSTORM IN ABERDEENSHIRE.—Mr Walter Long, M.P., Minister of Agriculture, had his attention directed to the devastation wrought in the Banchory district of Aberdeenshire by the great hailstorm at the beginning of August, has expressed the hope that the losses will be to some extent met by the efforts which have been made in the neighbourhood on behalf of the farmers and crofters who suffered through the disaster.
