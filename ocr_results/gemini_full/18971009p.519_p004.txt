BENNETTS, EDWARD EARL, timber merchant, St. Mawes.—The first meeting of the creditors concerned under this failure was held last week at the offices of the Official Receiver, Plymouth. The summary of accounts filed by the debtor disclosed unsecured liabilities amounting to £384, and assets estimated to produce £61. The deficiency after deducting the claims of preferential creditors payable in full, was returned at £325. The debtor attributed his failure to have been caused through losses on building contracts, depression in trade, want of capital, and sickness of himself, wife, and family. He stated that he commenced business in 1892 with only £10 capital. The Official Receiver stated that the largest debt (viz., £245) was in respect of timber supplied to the debtor. In April last the debtor gave the timber merchants to whom this sum was due under a contract. The Official Receiver said he thought he would have to go fully into that transaction, but in any case it would materially alter the deficiency.

HUGHES, GEORGE, contractor, Queenstown.—The debtor was publicly examined last week at the Chester Bankruptcy Court, before the Registrar Giles. The statement of affairs filed by the debtor disclosed liabilities amounting to £1,411, and the assets were expected to produce £333, thus leaving a deficiency of £1,078. Replying to questions put by the Official Receiver, the debtor stated that he had not been able to discover the whole of his books, which he alleged had been removed from his office whilst the bailiffs were in possession, and he had experienced considerable difficulty in preparing the accounts for the purpose of the Court. He admitted that all the cash included in the receipts as yet had been realised by the sale of his effects. He estimated he had lost at least £100 by the erection of eight houses at Queenstown. The debtor was then examined at considerable length by Mr. W. H. Churton, who appeared for the trustee, and severely cross-examined him in regard to the books and also with reference to certain goods which had been pawned for £28, in order that he might pay the men's wages on May 1st. The debtor stated that the articles in question consisted of jewellery, plate, &c., and some furniture belonging to his wife. He was not aware that the articles were pledged by his wife in her husband's name. The statement that he was in the possession of his wife's furniture was not true that they had only been on his premises just before the bailiffs went in. Mr. Abrahams had taken the furniture away, and his wife declined to allow any portion of the goods to be handed over to the trustee. He did not know what they were worth £40. Debtor was then questioned with regard to a Sunday-school he had built for the Primitive Methodists at Queenstown. The work considered cost him £200, and for it he had received nothing. He admitted that he must have been insolvent at the time, and did not think he should ever become solvent.
