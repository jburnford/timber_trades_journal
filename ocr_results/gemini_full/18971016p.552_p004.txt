GAZETTE.
FAILURES AND ARRANGEMENTS.
[It must not be overlooked that in many cases creditors whose names are published in these lists hold security for their debts, sometimes for the full amount.]

HICKS, JOHN THOMAS, builder, 7, Cranbrook Road, Ilford.-The public examination of this debtor took place at the Chelmsford Bankruptcy Court on the 7th inst. The statement of affairs showed liabilities expected to rank amounting to £1,628 6s. 10d., and the assets, after deducting the claims of preferential creditors, were estimated to produce £1 7s 4d.
The debtor stated, in answer to questions, that he commenced business as a painter and decorator in Camden Town about 25 years ago. About 14 years back he removed to his present address, and he started speculative building in March, 1896, although he had never had any previous experience in the trade. Mr. Booth: Do you think you were justified in speculating to such a large extent?-Debtor: I don't know, Sir. I had some little insight into building.-You have seen the result of your speculations?-I am sorry to say I have. I tried my best.-Were you justified in losing £1,600? What was your object in building?-Well, I had not very much work in hand at the time I started.-Debtor stated that when he commenced he had about £120 in cash to his credit. He built under agreements and lost on the first of these, but he went on because he thought he should improve. He had built thirty-four houses and one shop in East Ham. The houses cost £170 each to build, and the freehold was £52 10s, but he sold them for £210. He estimated that he lost about £27 on each house. He was obliged to sell to meet the demand of his creditors. At Ilford he built fourteen houses, and he estimated that he lost fully £25 each on two, and £10 on twelve. He did not keep proper books, because he had not time.-Mr. Booth: Is it not true that you drew considerably more money from the bank for wages than you needed for that purpose? Debtor explained that he might have drawn more, but it was for no other reason than to pay for odds and ends connected with the business, such as mould and sand. He attributed his failure to lack of experience, to having to sell at forced prices, and to the increase in the price of building materials. He had estimated the amount of his debts. Further questioned, debtor admitted that a stationery business was started about fourteen years ago by his wife, who still carried it on. His wife obtained the money by having let lodgings. He and his daughter also carried on a branch post-office. The commission amounted to about £10 a year at first, and had now increased to £20. Two of the creditors questioned the debtor, one, Mr. Smith, inquiring if he thought it honourable to have still gone on obtaining material from his creditors when he knew he had been losing for over a year. The examination was adjourned for the debtor to furnish further particulars of his cash account.

HOLLAND, JOHN, coachbuilder, 32, Victoria Road, Battersea.-This bankrupt attended at the Wandsworth Bankruptcy Court last week for his public examination. The liabilities are £2,900 8s. 4d., of which £803 14s. 2d. is expected to rank for dividend, and assets £841 14s. 9d. Bankrupt said he filed his petition in consequence of Messrs. Moser pressing. Messrs. Hopton had served a writ, and he had paid it out; but this came to Messrs. Moser's knowledge, and they pressed. At the date of the receiving order the High Bailiff was in possession. Although he had signed a declaration, and he could not pay his creditors, his sworn statement showed a surplus. He started at 122, Pimlico Road, which was an old-established business, having been founded by his grandfather in 1800. It had always been a success. He was obliged to leave Pimlico Road two and a half years ago, and he purchased the freehold of his present premises for £1,900, of which he paid £100 (which he obtained from the other business), and raised the balance on mortgage. He had expended about £600 on the premises, but that was in consequence of the frost, &c. He considered he placed £400 extra value on the freehold. It was mortgaged for £1,900. Nearly all his liabilities were due to trade creditors. His statement of affairs was prepared by his clerk. He placed the value on the stock, fixtures, &c., at what he considered they would be worth to himself, and not what they would realise. He had stated that the realisable value would be about half what he had stated. Eventually the examination was adjourned.

JONES, JOHN, timber haulier and contractor, Maesgoch, Manorcleifi, Llandilo.-The examination of this debtor was held before Mr. Registrar Parkinson, at the last sitting of the Carmarthen Bankruptcy Court. The statement of affairs filed by the debtor showed liabilities amounting to £473, and assets estimated to produce £444, thus leaving a deficiency of £29. The debtor was allowed to pass.

KENT, JAMES, late builder, of Haywards Heath.-The public examination of the debtor took place before Mr. Registrar Jennings at the Brighton Bankruptcy Court on the 7th inst. The debtor was questioned at considerable length by Mr. Mackintosh on behalf of the trustee, Mr. R. J. Ward, with regard to some houses in Gower Road, Haywards Heath, claimed by his wife. The debtor said he did not negotiate for the sale of the land, although he advised his wife to buy it. He did not find the money, and he did not actually pay the price. He supplied the material for the houses built on the land, and was paid by his wife. He did not receive any particular orders for the material, but supplied them as they were wanted. The houses have since been sold. He negotiated the sale, and agreed to the price on behalf of his wife. There was an advance of £150 by the bank. He could not say whether the money was paid to his account or not, but the loan was a joint one, and was paid off when the property was sold. There was a profit of £130 on the transaction which went to his wife. He admitted that whilst his own business was unfortunate in its results, his wife had been very fortunate in her speculations. He also admitted that he had bought some of his books, but that was because he thought they would be of no use to the Official Receiver. He had others at home which he would hand over. In 1891 he bought Norman House as agent for his wife, and afterwards sold it at a profit. His wife bought Salisbury House in December last for £55 at an auction sale. In September, 1889, he purchased a brickfield, the agreement being to pay £35 down, and the remainder at Christmas, but subsequently he agreed to pay off the balance by instalments of £100 per annum. He had paid £50 and five per cent. interest on the balance. The account was generally a matter of gross account. Just before his bankruptcy the vendor of the brickfield asked him to complete his purchase, and as he was unable to do so, the brickfield was seized and sold to witness's father-in-law, Mr. Mackintosh said that order that the books referred to might be produced, the examination must apply for an adjournment in order that the books referred to might be produced. The Registrar consequently adjourned the examination.

LAMB, JOSEPH, wheelwright, Wymeswold.-The public examination of the above-named debtor came on for hearing at the Leicester Bankruptcy Court last week. In answer to questions put by the Official Receiver, the debtor said that he had failed twice before in the same line of business. It had been a failure all along. The first time he failed was about fourteen years ago, and the second about eight years ago. The solicitor he engaged told him that he had received his discharge on the last occasion, but he had since found that was not so.
