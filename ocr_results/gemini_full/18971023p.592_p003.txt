The Receiver, who presided, stated that the liabilities were expected to rank at £1,650. The assets were estimated to produce £1,200. There were preferential claims, including rent, rates, wages, &c., amounting to £1,250, which had to be deducted from the assets. The debtor was questioned by the Official Receiver, and stated that he commenced business in 1888, with a capital of £400 unsecured creditors, all of which were in respect of goods supplied and work done. There were no creditors partly secured, and only one fully secured, that being a loan to the debtor for a loan of about £500. FIELD, WILLIAM, builder, Wick, Gloucester.—This debtor, who had carried on business as Field & Son, appeared for his public examination at the Bristol Bankruptcy Court, before Mr. Registrar Harley, on the 15th inst. Replying to questions put by the Official Receiver he stated that he had traded as Field & Son, but his son was not actually in partnership with him. His son had assisted him in his business. His present position was attributable to a loss of £200 on a contract at Stanton and to the loss, on another business, at Warmley, of £150. The examination was adjourned until November 5th.

A. F. FRAMPTON, LIMITED Poole and Bournemouth.—This company was formed in 1896 with a capital of £50,000, £20,000 of which was in preference shares and £30,000 in ordinary shares. When the old Weymouth Bank failed in the spring of 1897, and it was known that the above company owed the bank some £60,000, it was thought that when the Trustee in Bankruptcy of the bank began to realise its securities some difficulties might arise. On Friday, the 15th inst., when at a statutory meeting of shareholders it was decided to wind up the company. The circular sent out to the unsecured creditors, who are between £7,000 and £8,000, announces the winding up of the company, and states that "it is expected that no ultimate loss will fall upon the creditors." At the meeting it was thought that the preference shareholders (£20,000) might get 10s. in the pound, in which case there will be nothing for the ordinary shareholders. Foreign shippers are not among the list of creditors, as nothing in wood goods has been imported this season, sellers having been very chary in selling or in completing contracts. A large quantity of speculative building will be thrown upon the market shortly, when a full realisation takes place, and this will tend further to depress the wood trade generally in the Poole and Bournemouth neighbourhood.

OGDEN, W. S., late timber merchant, late of Cardiff and Bristol.—At the Cardiff Bankruptcy Court on Friday last the public examination of the above-named debtor was set down for hearing, and when the case was called on the Official Receiver said he, the debtor, was formerly managing director of a Cardiff firm of timber importers in a large way of business, but he had not yet filed a statement of affairs. The Registrar: Is he in attendance? The Official Receiver: He is not, therefore, I must ask for an adjournment. The Registrar: Why is he not present to-day? The Official Receiver: I do not know that anybody appear for the firm of Ivor Vachell & Co. said he appeared for the debtor, who had been in Canada for the benefit of his health. He was now in London for the purpose of obtaining certain documents. The Registrar: What is the good of his coming back from Canada if he does not appear on the day of his public examination? Mr. Roe: I cannot say. He had notice of the day. When I knew he was in London I notified him of the fact. I am sure that his non-appearance is not the result of advice on our part, and it is not out of disrespect for the Court. The Registrar: I do not suggest for one moment that it is, but I shall adjourn this case sine die.
