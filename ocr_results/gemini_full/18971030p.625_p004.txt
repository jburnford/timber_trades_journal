GAZETTE.
FAILURES AND ARRANGEMENTS.
[It must not be overlooked that in many cases creditors whose names are published in these lists hold security for their debts, sometimes for the full amount.]
ELLIOTT, SAMUEL.—The bankrupt, Samuel Elliott, who formerly traded as a joinery manufacturer at Broad Street, Golden-square, and Newbury, failed in March, 1895, and on Thursday applied at the London Bankruptcy Court for an order of discharge. The ranking liabilities were returned at £65,341, but proofs amounting to £117,531 have been made by creditors, and the assets, which were valued at £8,553, are stated to have realised only £3 788. It appeared from the debtor's statement that for about thirty-seven years prior to the date of the receiving order he traded at Newbury as a builder and contractor, but for the last eight years of the period as a joinery and moulding manufacturer. During the latter part of the trading he also had a London office at Broad Street House. During the six years immediately preceding the receiving order he was mainly engaged in developing and exploiting certain patented inventions for the annihilation of smoke and fumes. The bankrupt ascribed his failure to losses in respect of the inventions, and estimated that three-fourths of his liabilities were incurred in connection therewith. The application was opposed by the Official Receiver on various grounds. Mr. Chapman appeared as Assistant-Receiver, and Mr. Webb on behalf of the bankrupt. The Registrar said that 700 or 800 writs appeared to have been served upon the bankrupt in the space of five years, and about 500 executions levied during the same period. The natural inference was that the bankrupt was in a state of insolvency, and that he had gone on trading with a knowledge of his position. He was in the habit of giving bills for large amounts, especially as security for his debts, and also to accept accommodation bills. For the last four years from the date of the receiving order the bankrupt had been a director of a public company, and had received £1,000 a year as managing director. The Registrar said that the bankrupt had been guilty of rash and hazardous speculations, and had contracted debts without any reasonable prospect of being able to pay them. He had also brought on his bankruptcy by unjustifiable extravagance in living, and had put his creditors to unnecessary expense by a frivolous and vexatious defence to an action. The discharge was suspended for four years.
ELMORE, JAMES, builder and contractor, Seacombe.—This debtor was examined at the last sitting of the Birkenhead Bankruptcy Court, before the Registrar. The statement of affairs filed by the debtor showed liabilities amounting to £921, unsecured creditors, and assets estimated to produce £1,304, the latter consisting principally of the equities of his properties. In answer to questions, the debtor stated that he had been in business for a number of years. He had built properties in various parts of the district, on which he had obtained mortgages and created chief rents. About three years

ago he became a party to some Chancery proceedings, brought to restrain him from getting clay on some land at Seacombe, and he was committed to Walton Gaol for contempt of court in not obeying an order made by the Court. He was still in prison, and he alleged his failure to have been caused through that. After being questioned with regard to a bill of sale, the examination was adjourned until the next Court.
HOPKINS, PETER, timber merchant, District of Swansea, and Carmarthen.—The adjourned public examination of this debtor took place at the Swansea Bankruptcy Court before Mr. Registrar Home, last week, when debtor was questioned at some length with regard to certain accommodation bill transactions into which he had entered with persons in humble positions. Debtor alleged that his son attended to that part of the business. The Official Receiver pointed out that the debtor's son was dead, and could not answer for himself; in addition to that the son was only a clerk, in debtor's employ, so that debtor was the person responsible for the transactions. He admitted that in the aggregate the bills were for a large sum of money. The bank understood they were for goods received by the persons who gave the bills. They did not know that they were given by people in his employ, until last February. He admitted having told the manager of the Glamorganshire bank that his liabilities amounted to £4,869 and his assets to £14,000, and that a month afterwards when he called his creditors together, he presented a statement of affairs showing liabilities amounting to £11,000, and assets estimated to produce £2,000. The examination was further adjourned.
HUGHES, OWEN, trading as Owen Hughes and Son, joiners and builders, 47, Thomas Street, Liverpool.—The public examination of the above-named debtor took place last week at the Liverpool Bankruptcy Court, before Mr. Registrar Bellringer. The statement of affairs filed by the debtor showed liabilities amounting to £1,215, and assets estimated to produce £300. Replying to questions put by the Official Receiver, the debtor stated that his son was not a partner in the business. He took him into the business, and called the firm Owen Hughes and Son, as soon as the boy left school. His object was to encourage the lad, and not to trade under false pretences. In 1885 he made a private arrangement with his creditors, under which a deed of assignation was executed. His present difficulties had been mainly caused through losses on contracts, and to an award in an arbitration going against him. He was the plaintiff in the arbitration. The arbitrator was an architect, and was brother-in-law of the defendant, but the architect was named as arbitrator, in the contract, in case of dispute. He admitted that he was £236 to the bad in September of last year. His personal drawings from the business had only amounted to about 30s. per week. —The Official Receiver said he did not propose to question the debtor any further that day, but would leave it until another occasion, as the trustee had only just been appointed, and consequently he had not had time to go through the debtor's affairs.—After the debtor had been questioned by Mr. Glover, who appeared for the petitioning creditor, the examination was ordered to be adjourned to the next Court.
TIPPING, JOHN, builder, Liscard.—The public examination of the above named debtor came on for hearing at the Birkenhead Bankruptcy Court on the 21st inst. The statement of affairs filed by the bankrupt disclosed unsecured liabilities amounting to £1,026, gross liabilities £13,955, and assets consisting principally of equities of redemption, estimated at £2,301 4s. Replying to questions put by the Official Receiver, the debtor stated he made a private arrangement with his creditors in 1887. He then owed about £1,000 and under a deed of assignment, which he executed for the benefit of his creditors, a composition of 5s. 2½d. in the pound was paid to all who signed the deed. After that failure he commenced to build houses in Liscard and Wallasey, and he had been building in the district ever since. He only had about £20 worth of plant when he commenced the second time. The reason he

described himself as a speculative builder was that he built houses on advances and without any capital of his own. He had often received offers for his equities, but would not accept them. The properties had been in the hands of the collecting agents for a long time. The owner of the land at Wallasey had possession upon it. He sold his last house about six weeks ago, to pay wages. He had been a member of the Wallasey District Council. The household furniture belonged to his wife, it having been purchased for her at the time of the assignment, ten years ago. Notwithstanding the fact that £12,000 had passed through his hands, he had never kept a cash book, and he had no record of what he had done with the money, beyond his pass book. The Official Receiver said he should have had a cash account for the last two years. The debtor was an intelligent man, and had occupied a public position, and yet he alleged that he had not kept any record of his cash transactions during the last two years. Debtor said he had always paid the money away as he received it. Replying to Mr. Hime, who appeared for the petitioning creditors, the debtor stated that his wife was the tenant of the house in which he lived. He gave her £2 per week for housekeeping. Eventually the examination was ordered to be adjourned until the next court, the debtor in the meantime being requested to file a cash account.

WILSON, JOHN, of Erskine Street, Hulme, Manchester.—A meeting of creditors was held at Manchester on the 19th inst., Mr. Holford Harrison (of Harrison, Robinson, & Co.) being in the chair, when the following particulars were elicited, viz.:—Liabilities to unsecured creditors, £1,814 10s. 8d. Assets.—Stock-in-trade, £2,428 18s. 9d.; machinery, trade fixtures and fittings, £490; book debts, £883 1s. 9d.; on contracts, £1,237 5s 9d.; total £5,083 1s. 9d. Less preferential claims for rent, rates, wages, &c. £85—£4,998 11s. 11d, thus showing an estimated surplus of £131 1s. 3d. In addition to the assets shown the debtor has an interest under the will of his late father which may be worth two or three thousand pounds, so that there seems every likelihood of the creditors being paid in full. It was eventually resolved to do or execute an assignment of his estate to Mr. Joseph H. Scott, of the Timber Trades Association, and Mr. Broderick, with Messrs. H.
