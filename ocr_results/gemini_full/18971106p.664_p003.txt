GAZETTE.
FAILURES AND ARRANGEMENTS.
[IT must not be overlooked that in many cases those names are published in this list are only for their debts, sometimes for the first time.]
BENNETT, WILLIAM, trading as Bennett Bros., cabinet maker, 16, Church Street, 15, Parker Street, 24, Paradise Street, and 42, Manesty Lane, Liverpool.—The debtor appeared at the Liverpool Bankruptcy Court, last week, for his public examination. The Assistant Official Receiver stated that the creditors had accepted a proposal of the Court, and a scheme of arrangement whereby they would be paid 20s. in the £, therefore, under those circumstances, it was not it hardly necessary to question the debtor at any length. The debtor, in reply to questions, said he had filed a statement of affairs, showing liabilities amounting to £1,000, and assets estimated to produce £650. He admitted that the sheriff's officer had levied a considerable number of executions on the premises during recent years, and at the time of the receiving order there were as many as eighteen executions out against him. Possibly there were twenty. His present position had been caused through want of available capital and pressure by his creditors. The debtor was then allowed to pass his examination.

MOSS, SAMUEL, timber merchant and saw-mill proprietor, carrying on business at Gloucester.—This debtor came up at the Gloucester Bankruptcy Court for a first examination. The unsecured liabilities amounted to £1,698 10s. 10d., and the assets were estimated by the bankrupt at £606 5s. 6d., or, having regard to the realisation so far, a little over £400. He had previously failed in 1881, when his liabilities were over £16,000. Since that time he had gradually rebuilt up a large business, but a fire last year, and several bad debts brought about his failure. One of the matters which contributed to this was an action he had brought at Birmingham. He had contracted to supply a number of wood blocks, and he brought an action on the ground of misrepresentation, and it was continued when the result went against him. The blocks were at the present time in the railway station. Debtor was allowed to pass his examination.

OBORNE, FREDK. WM., builder, 54, Shaftesbury.—At the Salisbury County Court last week, before his Honour Judge Philbrick, Q.C., Mr. J. Trevor Davies applied for the discharge from bankruptcy of this debtor. He said that applicant had a fire at his premises, and, not being fully insured, lost a considerable amount of money. He called his creditors together, and they agreed to take 2s. 6d. in the £ on their claims. After that he tried to get his discharge, but failed. Mr. Trevor Davies then went through the books kept by his client, a long discussion ensuing between him and the Judge. In his decision his Honour said that he was strict enough to require a man to have everything kept in a particular form, either by double entry, or any advanced system of book-keeping, but at any rate they should have books to show, day by day, the cash coming in and going out. He had said it before, and he repeated that he would have to say it again, that he sat in the Court, that it ought to be made a criminal offence not to keep a proper set of books. He thought that they were unduly legislated not to have such a law, for nearly every civilised country had a law which made it a criminal offence if traders failed to keep proper books. The necessity of keeping proper books was indicated when a bankrupt applied for his discharge. In the present case, after making allowance for the unfortunate position he was placed in by the loss incurred at the fire, and the badness of trade, but apparently kept no cash book, nor did the clerk who appeared to make out the accounts. Payments were made by cheque, but the moneys received were made by cheque and cash, and he could not think that proper books of accounts were kept. He should suspend the discharge for two years.

OXFORD J F, builder, of 31, P[?] Road, Plumstead. S.E.—On Tuesday, at the Greenwich Bankruptcy Court, under a receiving order made on the debtor's own petition, the first meeting of creditors was held. The Official Receiver for the district, Official Receiver Hart, presided. The debtor, under a receiving order dated the 26th ult., issued particulars of his affairs. The debtor has been adjudged a bankrupt, and the first meeting of creditors has been adjourned to Tuesday next, at 24, Railway Approach, London Bridge, at 2 o'clock, when a trustee will be held as and the public examination will be held at the Greenwich Court on the following Tuesday. No resolutions were passed. An order for the summary administration of the estate has been made. He is not aware of his insolvency above a year ago.
