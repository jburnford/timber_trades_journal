FIELD, WILLIAM, builder, The Green, Wick, Glos.—The above-named bankrupt appeared for his adjourned public examination at the Bristol Bankruptcy Court, on Friday, before Mr. Registrar Harley, and after a few questions of minor importance had been put to him, the examination was again adjourned until December.
FREESTONE, WILLIAM, builder, Chepstow Road, Newport, Mon.—The first meeting of the creditors concerned under this failure took place last week, at the offices of the Official Receiver, Newport, Mon., when a statement of affairs was presented showing a deficiency of £415. As there was no offer of composition submitted to the creditors, it was decided to wind up the estate in bankruptcy, under the supervision of the Official Receiver. The debtor appeared for his public examination at the Newport Bankruptcy Court on Friday, on the application of the Official Receiver it was ordered to be adjourned until the next Court.
HINCHLIFFE, ALFRED, late joiner and cabinet maker, and builder, late Little Moor, Pudsey, now residing at 31, Brunswick Road, Pudsey, now residing at 31, Brunswick Bleachardshaw Lane, Pudsey.—The Official Receiver for the Bradford district has now issued particulars under this failure, from which it appears that the debtor has filed a statement of affairs showing gross liabilities amounting to £3,886, of which £2,300 is expected to rank against the estate for dividend. The assets are estimated to realise £402. The debtor alleges his failure to have been caused through losses in connexion with some real property, bad debts, and interest on borrowed money. The report and observations of the Official Receiver are to the following effect. The debtor, in 1891, commenced business with a capital of £250, borrowed money. He states that, with the exception of the second year's trading, his operations have been carried on at a continual and increasing loss. According to the debtor's deficiency account, his bad debts amounted to £200. Interest on borrowed money £350, and a further sum of £200 was accounted for as losses through speculative building at Pudsey. An unsuccessful attempt was made by the debtor to make a private arrangement with his creditors before filing his petition in bankruptcy.
HUGHES, OWEN, joiner and builder, Thomas Street, Liverpool.—The adjourned public examination of this debtor was held before Mr. Registrar Bellinger, at the Liverpool Bankruptcy Court on Thursday, last week. Mr. G. H. Thompson, who appeared for the trustee, questioned the debtor at some length with regard to a number of contracts which he had on hand at the date of the receiving order, and also in regard to the moneys he had borrowed. One item of £40 he stated he had borrowed at a money lending office near the Custom House, carried on in the name of the lender, but he afterwards discovered that the real lender was Mr. Isaac Gordon, of Birmingham. In respect of that loan of £40 he gave a bill for £60, payable by instalments extending over a period of twelve months. He did not receive the £40 in full, there being some small deductions for expenses. He had not paid all the instalments, and Mr. Gordon was still a creditor. Mr. Thompson asked for an order on the bank to file particulars of all contracts on which he had been engaged for the twelve months immediately preceding the date of the receiving order, the amounts paid, and the work done on each contract, and the names of the architects, and if no architects the names of the surveyors. The Registrar ordered the debtor to file the particulars asked for, and the examination was then further adjourned.
JOSEPH MATTHEWS, builder and soapmaker, Warrington.—The adjourned public examination of the above-named debtor took place on Friday, at the Warrington Bankruptcy Court, before Mr. Registrar Ridgway. The Official Receiver stated that since the previous sitting of the Court certain books and papers had been handed in by the debtor, but the cash account was wrong and would have to be amended. The account was wrong to the extent of £700, owing to certain items having been omitted. He asked that the examination might be adjourned until the December sitting of the Court, in order that the accounts might be amended, and to give the trustee an opportunity of thoroughly investigating the debtor's affairs. —The debtor on being asked why he did not hand over all his books to the trustee in the first place, replied that the trustee's clerk told him that they were of no importance.—The examination was then ordered to be adjourned.
OGDEN, WILLIAM SUTCLIFFE, late timber merchant, and director of John Bland & Co., Limited, timber importers, Cardiff, now residing at St. Leonard's-on-Sea, formerly carrying on business at Cardiff and Bristol.—The adjourned first meeting of the creditors interested under this failure was appointed to be held on the 5th inst. at the Official Receiver's Offices, Cardiff, but as there was not a quorum present the proceedings were purely formal, no resolutions being passed. The statement of affairs filed by the debtor disclosed unsecured creditors amounting to £6,443 11s. 5d., but there were fully secured creditors.
