CHIVERS, THOMAS JOHN, builder,
the Beehive Inn, Dale Street, late of Knowle,
Bristol.—The statutory meeting of the creditors
concerned under this failure took place on
the 18th inst. at the offices of the Official
Receiver, Bristol. The summary of accounts
disclosed liabilities amounting to £2,348, and
the assets were estimated to produce a surplus
of £538 over that amount. It appeared that
the debtor had been in difficulties on a previous
occasion, having failed in Bath in 1888,
when a dividend of 1 1/2d. in the pound was paid
to the unsecured creditors, whose claims
amounted to something like £1,000. Under
that bankruptcy he obtained his discharge.
He commenced business in Bristol
about two years ago with a capital of £100
lent him by his wife. He alleged his failure to
have been caused through inability to dispose
of house property built by him during the last
two years. His wife was the licensee of the
Beehive, where he was now residing. The
Official Receiver said he did not think there
would be much surplus from the securities in
the hands of fully secured creditors. The
estate was left in the hands of the Official
Receiver for summary administration.
HOPKINS, PETER, timber merchant,
Swansea.—The adjourned public examination
of this debtor was appointed to be held at the
next sitting of the Swansea Bankruptcy Court
before the Registrar, but when the case was
called on, Mr. Edwin Davies applied that it
might be further adjourned, as the debtor had
failed to complete the amended accounts
ordered at a previous sitting of the Court. The
Registrar granted the application, and informed
the debtor that he would have to pay more attention
to the orders of the Court, otherwise he should
have to report the debtor's conduct to the
Judge.
HUTCHINGS, JOHN, formerly timber
merchant, of Neath.—The adjourned public
examination of this debtor was held before Mr.
Registrar Charles at the last sitting of the
Swansea Bankruptcy Court, when the Official
Receiver stated that the debtor had not filed
his accounts to be required. The examination
was ordered to be adjourned sine die.
