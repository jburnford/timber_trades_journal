THE TIMBER TRADES JOURNAL.
Mackellar & Macphee,
Railway Yard, Edinburgh Dock, on Tuesday, the 23rd inst. There was the usual attendance of town buyers, and there was a good representation from the country, which materially helped the sale as regards prices, and a large number of lots were disposed of, but being in small quantities the total overturn must have been below an average. What was sold consisted chiefly of Baltic deals and battens, yellow pine deals and boards, and pitch pine deals, also mahogany and hardwoods being passed in the absence of satisfactory bids, although some business was reported in those goods as done privately after the close of the sale.
No absolute decision has yet been taken by the Dock Commissioners regarding the extension of the new dock at present under construction. The deputations from the Leith Chamber of Commerce, Shipowners' Society, and Cora Trade Association were received by the Dock Commissioners on the 19th inst., and a courteous hearing was accorded to their statements regarding the necessity of the proposed extension, but a decision was postponed until next meeting. The case for the timber trade was stated by Mr. J. T. Salvesen and Mr. D. W. Beattie, the latter on behalf of the timber importers, and the former on behalf of steamship owners engaged in the wood-carrying trade, increased facilities in connection with which are much required here.

SIR JAMES MARWICK AND THE BOARD OF AGRICULTURE.
When Sir James Marwick was in London he called on the Board of Agriculture to ascertain whether there is any likelihood of an early date of foreign animals being landed at Glasgow without being subject to slaughter at the port of landing. In reply, the Board stated that since the Diseases of Animals Act of 1896 came into operation they have no power to at any time sanction the admission of animals from foreign countries without the condition of slaughter at the port of landing, and that if, therefore, the accommodation at the Foreign Animals Wharf at Pointhouse, in the opinion of the local authority, is likely to afford sufficient space for the foreign animal trade of the port of Glasgow, there would seem to be no advantage in the continued maintenance of the lairs at Shieldhall. Sir James's call on the Board of Agriculture was due to an intimation from the Clyde Trust that the eastern portion of the Shieldhall property, the tenancy of which is during the pleasure of the trustees, is to be utilised in connection with the increasing timber trade. A sub-committee of the local authority has been appointed to report after conferring with the trustees.
