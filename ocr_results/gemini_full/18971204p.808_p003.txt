AXTELL, THOMAS, joiner and builder, Edith Road and St. Aldate's, Oxford.—The public examination of this debtor took place before Mr. Registrar Walsh at the sitting of the Oxford Bankruptcy Court, when in reply to questions he stated that he had filed the accounts ordered at a previous sitting of the Court. He always paid small accounts in cash and passed them through the petty cash book. Cheques for wages were sometimes in excess of the amount actually required, and sometimes less, and the balance was invariably added to or taken from the petty cash. He had betted small amounts for some time past on the river and it was still a punt on the river. He had a total amount of his indebtedness about £12,000, which probably was at the rate of £10 per day during the time he carried on business. He was sure his books showed the amount of his losses on contracts. His biggest loss on a single contract was £2,000 on the Corn Exchange. He did not think his unfinished contracts had been finished at a profit. Eventually the examination was ordered to be further adjourned.

ROWE, MARTIN FREDERICK, builder and cycle agent, March and Wisbech.—A private meeting of the creditors interested under this failure, took place at the Bedford Hotel, Peterborough, under the presidency of Mr. Sharpe, manager to the Stamford, Spalding, and Boston Bank. The statement of affairs filed by the bankrupt disclosed liabilities to creditors in the building trade, £1,135 9s. 3d.; to cycle trade creditors, £833 17s. 7d.; total, £1,968 16s. 10d. To partly secured creditors, £948 10s.; total liabilities, £2,311 6s. 10d. Assets, consisting of stock of building material, £707 11s. 9d.; stock of cycles, &c., £263 7s. 1d.; good book debts, £125; horses, carts, harness, &c., £147 2s. 6d.; total assets, £1,243 1s. 5d. It appeared that the debtor commenced business about eight years ago without any capital of his own, and he has done a large business in contracts for the Great Eastern Railway Company over their system, but latterly, in consequence of keener competition, this business has fallen off considerably, and some two years ago he embarked in the cycle trade, he being a well known rider, and during the last two years he has acted as handicapper for the local Cyclists Union in that district. He alleged his failure to have been caused through insufficient capital and losses by bad debts. He had no offer of composition to submit to the meeting. After some discussion it was resolved by the meeting that the debtor should execute a deed of assignment for the benefit of his creditors generally, and that Mr. George Graham Poppleton, chartered accountant, of Messrs. Poppleton and Appleby, Birmingham, Huddersfield, and London, and Mr. J. Adnett, of Peterborough, be appointed trustees to wind up the estate under the deed, in conjunction with a committee of inspection consisting of five of the largest creditors.
SANDERSON, builder, Sudbury and Hanwell.—The first meeting of creditors in this case was held at the London Bankruptcy Court, on Monday, before Mr. A. H. Wildy, Official Receiver. The accounts showed unsecured debts £652 12s. 1d., and debts fully secured £709 10s. 10d.; against assets £196 4s. 2d. The debtor commenced business in September, 1894, with £45 capital. He attributes his failure to losses under a contract through increase in the price of building materials and to alleged illegal possession taken by the freeholder, also to bad debts, and to his inability to obtain tenants for houses that he had completed. In the absence of a quorum of creditors the meeting was adjourned for a week.

The debtor alleged his failure was caused through loss of speculative building, and excessive interest paid to money lenders, and the petitioning creditor, for whom he was held liable, through purchasing the goods for another person, for which he did not expect to realise any profit. The Chairman stated that the meeting had been adjourned because on the last occasion the proofs had not been stated to carry the offer of composition. The offer of composition was made by the creditor's son, who had agreed to pay the unsecured creditor's a composition of 10s. in the pound, payable by two instalments of 5s. each, at six and twelve months from the date of approval of same by the Court, in addition to which he had agreed to pay all the preferential creditor's claims and all other costs, charges, and expenses of and incidental to the proceedings, and all fees and percentages payable to the Official Receiver or the Board of Trade, in full, before an application was made to the Court, to confirm the composition. Mr. Willard, jun., has come forward with that generous offer, because he wished to save his father from the disgrace of being adjudged a bankrupt.—The Chairman, after reading over the proofs, said that before the composition could be carried, there would have to be a majority of the creditors in number and a three-fourths majority in value. There was a majority in number, and the amount required as regarded value to carry the composition was £460, but as a matter of fact there were creditors to the amount of £500 in favour of the offer of Mr. Willard, jun. therefore he should declare the composition carried.—Mr. Willard, jun., thanked the meeting for accepting his offer, and the proceedings were then brought to a close.
