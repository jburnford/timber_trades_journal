The list of arrivals this week is a short one, comprising 24,110 deals and battens, per “Cear” (s), from St. Petersburg, 8,183 battens, and 2,443 pit props, per “Pocohontas,” from Shien, 12,867 deals and battens, and 12,442 pcs. lathwood, per “Ennismore” (s), from Riga, 18,637 deals and ends, per “Dania” (s), from Montreal.
There has been a large importation of wood for the eleven months of the present year, and although the consumption has been also large, there is no doubt that stocks in importers’ hands are heavier than usual.
This is not, however, to be regretted, as there is a very considerable amount of work in prospect in the building trade, and with shippers still advancing their prices a light stock in hand would not be desirable.
With the advancing season and inclement weather the volume of business is perceptibly diminishing, and the dull time has set in.
The state of matters was reflected in the public sale held this week by Mr. D. W. Beattie, at the Edinburgh Dock, the attendance of buyers being small, and the business done correspondingly restricted.
No disposition was shown, however, towards weakness in prices, and for what was sold fairly remunerative rates were obtained.

DECEMBER 4, 1897.
GLASGOW CATHEDRAL.
Mr. MacGregor Chalmers, president of the architectural section of the Philosophical Society, recently delivered the opening address of the session.
There was a large attendance of the members and friends.
The subject of the address was “Notes taken from a Sketch of the History of Glasgow Cathedral,” a sketch which the president intimated he had now in preparation for publication.
Dealing first with the connection of Glasgow with the three leaders in the Christianising of Scotland—St. Ninian, St. Columba, and St. Mungo—a brief description was given of the early plans of churches, both of the Roman and the Celtic types.
