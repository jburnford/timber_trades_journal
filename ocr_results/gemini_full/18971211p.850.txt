EMPLOYERS' LIABILITY.
ADAMS V. M'GREGOR.
In the Glasgow Court of Session recently, issues were ordered for trial by jury of an action raised in the Dumbarton Sheriff Court at the instance of Peter Adams, labourer, Lilybank Road, Dumbarton, against Andrew M'Gregor, wood merchant, Teang, Forfarshire. On 23rd August last, when in the employment of the defender, a saw-mill had been partially erected at the place. The pursuer, who had been only five days at the work, and had no previous knowledge of the employment, was assisting to pull them through to the bench, and then through the circular saw. They were sawing wood on to a plank which was used as a temporary footway from one end of the saw to the other, when the plank rose and he put out his hands to save himself from falling. The two centre fingers of his left hand were torn off. It is averred that the footway was defective. Defender says he believes that at the time of the accident the pursuer was the worse for liquor, and incapable of properly discharging the work he had undertaken. It is also averred that the kind of work pursuer was usually employed in was of the kind of a such work. The pursuer denies that he was worse for liquor.

DOCHERTY V. THE CLYDE NAVIGATION TRUSTEES.
In the Court of Session, Edinburgh, on the 2nd inst. issues were ordered for the trial of an action by Thos. Docherty, foreman stevedore, 62, Broomielaw Street, Glasgow, against the Clyde Navigation Trustees for £100 as damages for personal injuries. He was, on the 10th September last, employed by a firm of stevedores to assist in discharging oakwood batten from the s.s. "Orthia" in Cessnock Dock, and he avers that owing to the fault of the defenders' craneman, he was struck by a sling load and thrown down through a hatch into the hold, a distance of 30 feet. The pursuer attributes the accident to the fault of an incompetent craneman. Liability is denied by the defenders, who say that the pursuer, in attempting to get out of the way of the battens, overbalanced himself and fell into the hold.
