FAILURES AND ARRANGEMENTS.
[It must not be overlooked that in many cases creditors whose names are published in these lists hold security for their debts, sometimes for the full amount.]

BAUCKHAM, ROBERT, late saw-mill proprietor, Grimsby.—Mr. Barker made an application on behalf of the above-named debtor before his Honour Judge Shortt, at the Great Grimsby Bankruptcy Court, for his discharge. He stated that the receiving order was made about six years ago. Debtor was in partnership with his father, and they were very unfortunate in business. He was a shipwright by trade, but the partners carried on a saw-mill. Debtor only drew about £2 per week from the business. They lost a lot of money through a fishing smack.—The Official Receiver said he had two objections to make against the application, being that the debtor had not paid 10s. in the pound, and the other was that he had not kept proper books of account. He agreed that it was a case of misfortune, but he did not think the Court had power to suspend the discharge for less than two years. With regard to the fishing smack, he might mention that after they had purchased it they were dissatisfied with their bargain, and they offered the vendor £200 to release them from it. The debtor had not fully accounted for his deficiency, and the creditors had only received about 3½d. in the pound on the amount of their debts. Eventually his Honour suspended the discharge for two years.

FAHERTY, F. T., builder, 83, Alston Street, Birmingham.—At the first meeting of the creditors interested under this failure, held at the offices of the Official Receiver, Birmingham, a statement of affairs was submitted to the meeting showing liabilities amounting to £2,616 3s. 3d., of which £946 3s. 5d., was due to unsecured creditors, the deficiency being returned at £836 5s. 3d. It appeared from the Official Receiver's report that the bankrupt commenced business in 1890, borrowing £30. In April, 1894, he suspended payment, when his liabilities amounted to £499 5s. The creditors then accepted a composition of 7s. 6d. in the pound, and to enable him to pay it he borrowed £100 on a bill of sale. After that debtor found considerable difficulty in continuing his business. He also lost money on one or two of his contracts. In 1895 he commenced speculative building, erecting houses with funds advanced upon progressive mortgage. The Official Receiver understood that he was engaged on five of those undertakings. The debtor alleged his failure to have been caused through loss on trade and law charges. The bankrupt was entitled to a reversionary interest under his father's will, subject to his surviving mother, now aged about 72. It was mortgaged to the bill of sale holder for £340. During the last twelve months the debtor had been continually sued by his creditors. In July last the debtor was engaged in building three houses and a shop, upon which he had borrowed £360 on the mortgage, and he was served with notice to complete certain work within three days, otherwise the work would be taken out of his hands. He was unable to carry out the work, and the threat was carried out. Since then he has not carried on business. The whole of his assets were claimed under the bill of sale. Eventually the estate was left in the hands of the Official Receiver for summary administration in the usual manner.
