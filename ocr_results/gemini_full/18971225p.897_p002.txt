GAZETTE.
FAILURES AND ARRANGEMENTS.
[It must not be overlooked that in many cases creditors whose names are published in these lists hold security for their debts, sometimes for the full amount.]
BARTLETT, HENRY, builder, 61, Eaton Place, and 103, Street, carrying on business at 29, Castle Street, Brighton.—The summary of this debtor's statement of affairs discloses gross liabilities £4,119 9s. 1d. ; expected to rank, £4119 9s. 1d. ; net assets are as follows: Building plant, at 29, Castle Street, Brighton, £95 ; book debts, £2 7s. 6d. ; surplus from securities in the hands of creditors fully secured, £177 1s. 6d. ; total, £214 8s. 6d. ; less preferential claims, £11 5s. 6d. ; net assets, £203 3s. ; deficiency, £3,916 6s. 1d. The bankrupt alleges as the cause of failure, "Inability to realise my houses in Portland Road, No. 9, Portland Road, and Portland Road, which has been finished two years, particularly which has been practically no rent, and has not and produced practically no rent, and having to pay interest on mortgages." The Official Receiver's remarks are: The receiving order was made on the debtor's own petition on November 25th, 1897. Orders of adjudication for summary administration have also been made.—The bankrupt states that he commenced business as a jobbing builder 40 years ago. That in 1890 he built five houses in Portland Road, Hove, on speculation, but in consequence of some of the houses remaining unlet and his inability to sell them, he estimates a considerable loss, and to this he attributes his insolvency. No proper books of account have been kept. The bankrupt states that in 1892 he married. That his wife was then carrying on a lodging-house at 61, Preston Street, the whole of the furniture being her property. That she continued to carry on this business since the marriage, and now claims the whole of the furniture in the house ; that some years after the marriage the bankrupt purchased the leasehold but subsequently sold it, and then took a lease of the premises in his own name. He states that the rent has always been paid by the wife out of the earnings of the house. The bankrupt's summary of his statement of affairs is set out as follows :—

HOPKINS, PETER, late timber merchant, Swansea, Llandyssil, and Carmarthen.— The adjourned examination of this debtor was held at the Swansea Bankruptcy Court, before Mr. Registrar Home, last week, but no further of importance transpired, and the examination was formally adjourned to the next Court, when it will be finally closed.
JAY, GEORGE, builder, 9, Marlborough Road, Swansea.—This debtor was publicly examined before Mr. Registrar Home, at the last sitting of the Swansea Bankruptcy Court. The summary of accounts filed by the debtor disclosed liabilities amounting to £1,921, of which £1,660 was due to fully secured creditors and £818 was owing to unsecured creditors. The deficiency was estimated at £801. According to the Official Receiver, the debtor said his present position had been caused through depreciation in the value of his property, insufficient trade at saw mills to meet current expenses, and the destruction of the saw mills and stock by fire, also to losses as a speculative builder. He further stated that his premises were not insured when burnt down. The debtor was eventually allowed to pass his examination.
