CHICAGO. 
(From the “North-western Lumberman,” November 29th.) 

The lumber season has not been very brisk this week. The receipts 
have not decreased materially in least, but all the docks, as usual at 
this fall, are blocked up. The retail yards have good, constantly good 
trade, and the last of the builders are putting away a lacking amount 
for last winter and finds more lumberly and from about that the sum will 
17½ dollars per thousand feet. To every one selling lumber out of our 

yards the season just closing has been a decidedly satisfactory one. 
There has been an unusually large quantity of lumber received and a 
correspondingly good volume in the export trade, both in lake and rail. 
The shipments and local retail trade are up to the figures of last year. 
Freights have ruled higher, and there has been an advance in prices of 
most all the grades that come to this market. The Michigan shore 
has had a very good season, and the profits to the lumbermen have been 
larger than they were a year ago. 
 
The statistics of the Chicago lumber trade for 1885 are now being 
prepared, and will be brought up to the 1st January. 
The total receipts of lumber will reach 2,335,000,000 feet, being 
only 100,000,000 below last year.
