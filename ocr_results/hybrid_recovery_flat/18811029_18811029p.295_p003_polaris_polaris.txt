The Committee of the Landes (French), Departmental 
Council have decided to extend to Nov. 30th next the term 
for importation of undressed timber for pit-props, 
substituted for cut wood of all kinds. 

The expected sale of the D’Esterre estate, near 
Tralee, on the 30th inst., includes a snipe or pasturage 
letting. 

The timber bearing the initials “M.O.”, “W.B.”, and 
“W.B.M.,” is “registered” at Croydon. 

Some more saw mills are to be erected in the 
neighbourhood of Moscow, and at Vasili 
Bykov, on the Volga. 

Modules are issued now, by the West 
Yorkshire Railway, from the west of England 
to the Severn and Wye Railway, Mitcheldean 
Road (Forest of Dean), and Lydbrook Branches, 
for all goods, including timber, from Liverpool 
and Manchester.

proportions is reported at Civray. 

MR. DAVID C. GRAHAM, timber-broker, Glasgow, 
has been elected Deacon of the Incorporation of 
Masons of that city. 

A NEW timber company, to be called “The Yorkshire 
Carriage and Wagon Company (Limited), and Wooden 
Wagon Company, is about to be formed, to 
take over the works of an extensive manufacturer. 

A “TIMBER MERCHANT'S YARD.”—Mr. William G. 
Ward, timber merchant, of Crewe, has been summoned 
for keeping his timber yard in such a condition as to 
cause a nuisance, by allowing offensive water to 
flow therefrom into an adjoining yard. Defendant, 
who stated that it was the fault of the sanitary authority 
that the nuisance existed at all, was ordered to 
abate the nuisance and pay costs of court. 

A “GREAT DREADFUL.”—At Liverpool, recently 
the captain of a lighter laden with 500 barrels of 
flour, was charged with having caused the vessel to 
sink, and with having subsequently made away with 
the cargo of flour in order to defraud an insurance 
company. A witness stated that he “helped to hook 
some bags out of the water, but they were in a very 
damaged condition, some being reduced almost to 
flour and water,” and that after the lighter sank he 
did not expect he would receive any portion of the 
cargo. The man was committed for trial, the 
magistrates remarking that they “would not have been 
doing their duty if they had refused to send him 
for trial.”
