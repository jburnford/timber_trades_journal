AMERICAN AND CANADIAN ITEMS.

The latest private reports makes the John Ward [Ward?] of plain sawn; Red Island, 4, 5, and 7 in., now entirely cleared out, with no stocks whatever of these sizes remaining in any yard in St. John.

Van Buren in famine, regular Ban. 40,000 deals.

Osa, Dalsland, in famine, entirely free from blockade.

Osa, Soderman; just in new arrivals from 3,000 deals, and a quantity of lumber was also consigned.

A. H. Gilmour & Bros., lumbermen, of St. George, N.B., whose affairs have been in a complicated state for some time past, have assigned in trust. Nearly all their assets are covered by bills of sale and judgment, so that there will be no dividend to the general creditor or outside friends.
Montreal Times.

A future lumber region, says the Lumberman, lies in the route of the Northern Pacific Railway, from Montana eastward to the Rock Lake river, through forests of cedar and tamarac, 100 to 300 years old, and little disturbed by the Indians. Many of the trees are very large — 70 to 100 feet in height, and rising to great heights. Although the customer, so far, has been inconsiderable, yet, with better communication, it may without question, at no distant time be specially attracted to and furnish the greater thoroughfare of timber from which a great lumber industry will grow up throughout the region.
