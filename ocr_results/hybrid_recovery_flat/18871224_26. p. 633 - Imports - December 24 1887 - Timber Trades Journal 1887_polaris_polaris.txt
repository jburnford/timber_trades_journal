CORRESPONDENCE

AUSTRALIAN HARDWOODS.

TO THE EDITOR OF THE “TIMBER TRADES JOURNAL.”

SIR,—We observe in your issue, December 10, the letter of your Sydney correspondent, who states that “Karri, and Walsh, he would wish it understood, is not recommended,” but with reference to your own comments regarding that timber as a substitute for American “Redwood,” and that the same statements containing contradictions from the various authorities, dissolved by a mixture of statements put forward by interested parties, far too frequently without due investigation, have had the effect of seriously retarding the trade in so valuable and economically obtainable wood. In a few years hence we confidently anticipate Karri will be the most popular hardwood in use.

In contradiction to the certain persons who have received and reported on Karri, we would state that the West Australian Government some two years back had tests made by independent engineers throughout Melbourne and Sydney, regarding the various timbers without mentioning the certain persons who have received and reported on Karri, we would state that the West Australian Government some two years back had tests made by independent engineers throughout Melbourne and Sydney, regarding the various timbers, and the results, we believe, have been published.

Professor Lantelo, Esq., Timber Inspector to the Melbourne University and the largest (and oldest) timber inspector in Australia, states that Karri is 20 per cent, stronger than Jarrah. Karri’s tensile strength is taken at 1800 lb.; Jarrah, 1356 lb., the strength of Karri being 2 to 1 as against Jarrah.

Again as to Karri being heavier than Jarrah, the former weighs 73 lbs. per cubic foot; Jarrah, 71 lbs.; Karri splits in fibres 7 inches long; Jarrah, 2 inches.

With respect to the difference between sawn and hewn timber, a large number of claims have been submitted to the Government of Victoria, for compensation, for the use of Karri as paving blocks.

But for more than the space at our command, we could give numerous public testimonials to the same effect, as we understand it is now generally acknowledged both by scientists as well as the public, that previous adverse reports regarding Karri were not only unfounded, but prejudicial.

We further feel the growing requirements of Australian hardwoods in your country will with the proper knowledge be eventually filled by Karri, and we may also mention the Jacob Morton is the first vessel loaded entirely with Karri, and we have already published adverse and reliable opinions.

We are, Sir, your obedient servants,

Agents for the West Australia Karri & Jarrah Co.,

Wood, Wood, London, E.C.

1st December, 1887.
