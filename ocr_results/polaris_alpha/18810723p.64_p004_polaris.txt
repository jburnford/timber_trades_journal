CASUALTIES.  

Struck a Rock.—On Wednesday morning, while laden with timber,  
the barque Heather Bell, from Sundsvall, bound for London, is said  
to have struck on the Swin Spitway Lightship; a boat  
was at once dispatched to render assistance.  

Loss of the “Ada Lewis.”—The ship “Ada Lewis,”  
from New York to Liverpool, with a cargo of general merchandise,  
is reported lost, with a portion of her cargo saved.  

The Dazzling Rescue.—Yarmouth.—This  
port, Monday last, was the scene of another fine  
act of bravery at sea. Intelligence reached  
here that a vessel was ashore off Yarmouth, and  
a life-boat was at once launched. After a severe  
struggle with the heavy sea, the crew were rescued  
and landed. Every possible effort was made to save  
the vessel, but without success.  

Liverpool.—Intelligence has been received  
that the iron barque “Vernon” has been abandoned  
at sea. The crew were picked up by a passing  
vessel and safely landed. The “Vernon” was loaded  
with deals from a Northern port. The captain  
reports the vessel sprang a leak during heavy weather,  
and the pumps becoming choked, she had to be abandoned.  

The Wreck Register.—The official  
returns of wrecks for the past year show that although  
the number of casualties has slightly increased, there  
is a very marked diminution in the loss of life. This  
satisfactory result is mainly due to the efficiency of  
our life-boat service and the improved character of  
our coasting craft. It is, however, worthy of remark  
that a large number of the casualties occurred in con-  
sequence of overloading and improper stowage, and  
from the want of competent officers and men. The  
attention of shipowners is again called to the  
necessity of exercising greater care in the loading  
and manning of their vessels. The Board of Trade  
has also under consideration further regulations with  
regard to load-line and equipment.
