LEITH.
The arrivals of wood goods at this port
during the past week are still very light
consisting of 3 American cargoes from Que-
bec; another cargo is coming from Kron-
stadt of battens, sundry small parcels of
deals, hard wood, &c.
A considerable num-
ber of cargoes of wood are now coming for-
ward, which it is probable will soon bring
the values of that item to a lower level.
There is no change to report in the
condition of the market; there is generally a
good deal of business being done in the home
trade, especially by the larger
houses, but with comparatively long credit.
There is no inducement to keep shipping
deals, the freights from Hamburg, Dant-
zig, &c., being much too high.
Advices
from the Baltic report audits at the Imperial
Sawmill, Port Baltic and Memel showing
such a slackness for delivery, as already
to cause considerable annoyance, especially
at the yellow pine, and slated ends.
There
are still very few reports of operations to-
come from the interior, showing that opera-
tions of any magnitude were undertaken,
but that the stocks were very near clean,
and at high prices.
There were few stores
of one sort and other, and were cheap, for
a moderate supply.
There is no unusual
business doing either in Baltic yellow
pine or Quebec, or home hewn, values a
shade firmer.
Some further shipments of
soft woods were taken here for building
purposes.
Sawn pines, &c., are in fair
request.
Hewns are not so much inquired
for, but we notice a rise in Baltic deals and
a slight advance in prices.

ABERDEEN.
A large and representative gathering
connected with the timber trade in
the North responded to Messrs. Fleming
Brothers’ invitation on Wednesday, when
their new steam saw mill premises
were opened by the commencement of
milling, at the close of the sale Messrs.
Fleming Brothers entertained the whole
of those present at a dejeuner in the hand-
some suite of rooms of their new establish-
ment, the tasteful hall, Mr. Fleming, on
behalf of the partners, presided.
The food
good and patriotic toasts were proposed
and responded to, the band temperance being
specially honoured, and made favourable
all the timber trade.
This is now about three
years since they began, and without any desire
to boast of their success, and that of their
different branches, it would be Herford to
the opinion that their success be reckoned
a proof.
